## 1. Reflective Computing — research preprint

- **Author:** Md Haseen Akhtar
- **Title:** *Reflective Computing: Seven Dimensions from the Gaps in 134 Interactive Systems, 1996–2026*
- **Identifier:** arXiv:2610.35696v1 [cs.HC]
- **Submitted:** 26 October 2026
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35696
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35696
- **Accessed:** 2026-11-09
- **Type:** Single-author research preprint; arXiv submission. Do not describe it as peer-reviewed or published in a journal unless independently confirmed.
- **Review status:** Full 54-page PDF inspected at source level; a complete independent replication or audit of its underlying corpus and supplementary data has not been performed.

### Research question and contribution

The paper proposes reflective computing as a human-computer interaction paradigm that treats human reflection as the organizing aim of a system, rather than as a side effect of sensing, behaviour change, wellbeing or self-tracking.

It reports a critical review of a purposive set of 134 interactive systems from 17 ACM venues (1996–2026), followed by coding with two independent coders. The author derives seven dimensions:

1. **Tempo agency** — who controls the pace of interaction.
2. **Invitational form** — whether form invites engagement without coercion.
3. **Interpretive openness** — room for a person to make their own meaning.
4. **Reflective depth** — support beyond simple description.
5. **Temporal span** — connection across moments and time.
6. **Protected idleness** — space for pause and unfilled time.
7. **Ethical non-extraction** — accountable handling of data and inner life.

The abstract reports inter-coder reliability measures and comparative findings. Treat numerical findings as the paper's reported results, not independently reproduced facts. The sample is purposive rather than an exhaustive census of HCI publications; the paper itself states that its counts describe the selected exemplar set.

### Critical appraisal questions

- How are the seven dimensions operationalised in the codebook and supplementary materials?
- How were the 134 exemplar systems selected, and what traditions or venues may be underrepresented?
- What do the reported reliability measures establish, and what do they not establish?
- How are missing reports about data practice treated?
- Can the proposed dimensions guide design without becoming another score used to judge people or products?
- What would a participant-controlled, non-extractive evaluation look like in a local-first system?

**Working interpretation:** The strongest connection to IKAI is its insistence that reflection is a human capacity, not an automated system output. Tempo agency and ethical non-extraction align with IKAI's mirror-not-judge, participant-directed, local-first research boundary. This is a design interpretation, not an endorsement by the paper's author or proof that IKAI satisfies the framework.

## 2. Labanotation — movement notation reference

- **Title:** Labanotation
- **Supplied discovery source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labanotation
- **Accessed:** 2026-11-09
- **Source type:** Tertiary encyclopedia entry; discovery and orientation only.
- **Historical attribution in the entry:** Rudolf Laban's 1928 *Schrifttanz*; later development by practitioners including Albrecht Knust and Ann Hutchinson Guest.
- **Terminology:** Labanotation and Kinetography Laban are closely related traditions with historical and regional development differences; do not assume they are perfectly interchangeable in every technical or pedagogical context.

### Core notation concepts

The Wikipedia overview describes a staff-based system in which:
- symbol shape indicates direction;
- shading indicates level;
- symbol placement indicates the body part;
- symbol length indicates duration;
- vertical progression represents temporal sequence;
- additional signs can express dynamic qualities and spatial relationships.

The page distinguishes detailed Labanotation scores from Motif Description / Motif Notation, which is more selective and captures essential movement ideas rather than every detail.

### Corroborating institutional source

Ohio State University's Department of Dance resource page describes Labanotation (Kinetography Laban in Europe) as a structured symbolic system and outlines direction, level, timing and body-part placement. It separately describes Motif Description and Laban Movement Analysis (LMA), including Body, Space and Effort categories.

- **Institutional source:** https://dance.osu.edu/research/dnb/resources
- **Accessed:** 2026-11-09
- **Use:** More suitable than Wikipedia for an initial institutional orientation; follow its links to the Dance Notation Bureau, International Council of Kinetography Laban, LabanLab and Laban/Bartenieff Institute.
- **Important distinction:** Labanotation is a movement notation system; LMA is an analytical framework; Bartenieff Fundamentals is a related but distinct movement practice. They are not synonyms.

### Research and rights limits

Wikipedia is not an authority for detailed notation instruction. Consult original notation texts, institutional teaching materials, specialist scholarship and qualified practitioners before encoding or teaching the system. Do not reproduce copyrighted scores, symbols collections, diagrams, photographs or instructional materials without checking rights and permitted use.

## 3. Cross-domain relationship

| Domain | Relevant contribution | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| FELOH Corpus | Canonical source metadata, claim provenance and research governance | Does not turn a source summary into a verified implementation |
| IKAI | Participant-directed reflective interfaces, temporal reflection and non-extractive local computation | No diagnosis, identity inference, emotion/personality inference, scoring, ranking or covert profiling |
| GENJE | Movement scores, choreography, improvisation and embodied creative documentation | Do not reduce cultural practice or performers to sensor features or automated judgements |

The two sources meet at a useful research question: **How might a person revisit and interpret their own movement or creative process over time, while retaining control over pace, meaning, context and data?**

This is a research question, not a claim that Labanotation is itself reflective computing or that a notation score reveals a person's inner state.

## 4. Proposed research work packages

1. **Notation literacy:** study basic Labanotation and Motif notation with qualified instructional resources; document the differences between descriptive score and analytical interpretation.
2. **Embodied authorship:** examine how performers, choreographers and communities author, annotate, revise and interpret movement records.
3. **Reflective interface study:** prototype a participant-controlled journal for revisiting self-authored movement annotations; begin with manual entries and synthetic examples.
4. **Local-first data model:** specify export, correction, deletion, retention and provenance before any sensor ingestion.
5. **Evaluation:** test comprehension, participant control and interpretive openness; do not evaluate or rank the person.
6. **Cultural grounding:** document the specific dance, community, language, context and permissions. Do not generalise from European notation systems to African movement traditions without situated research.
