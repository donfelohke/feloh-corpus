## 3. Conceptual distinctions

- **Self-hosting:** operating a service under one's own administrative control.
- **Federation:** independently operated services exchange data through a common protocol.
- **Decentralisation:** control or function is distributed across multiple nodes or authorities; degree varies by design.
- **Peer-to-peer networking:** peers communicate without relying exclusively on a central service for the relevant function.
- **Community networking:** people collectively build, operate or govern connectivity infrastructure.
- **Cooperative ownership:** members hold defined rights and obligations under an institutional/legal structure.
- **Digital sovereignty:** practical capacity to understand, control, maintain, migrate and govern infrastructure and data.

None implies the others automatically. A self-hosted server can be controlled by one person; a federated service can be commercially operated; a mesh can still have centralised governance or one upstream dependency.

## 4. Cross-domain implications

### WARENA — capability

WARENA owns technical evaluation. OpenWrt/LibreMesh and GNUnet are reuse candidates, not prompts to build replacements. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, overlay networking, Tor, federation and USSD need distinct interfaces and threat models. Database selection follows workload evidence. GNU Taler is a payment protocol candidate, not governance.

The WARENA Technology Constitution excludes JavaScript and web technologies as application/runtime/integration architecture. ActivityPub is HTTP-based Web technology; a JavaScript-free implementation is not web-independent. Artanis and NGINX also require explicit boundary treatment.

### IKAI — reflective computing

IKAI may need local compute, reliable device I/O and resilient local storage. Cooperative infrastructure can provide connectivity and shared services, but reflective core functions should not require remote accounts or continuous upstream connectivity.

Any shared environment must disclose operator, data flows, retention, access, export/deletion and offline behaviour. Networked reflection must not become covert surveillance, biometric identity matching, person scoring or behavioural profiling.

### GENJE — expression

Community networks can support creator-controlled exchange of performance records, audio/video, movement notation, livecoding artefacts and collaborative research. Distribution must preserve authorship, credits, cultural context, licence, consent and provenance.

Federation/self-hosting is not permission to redistribute recordings, samples, choreography, images or participant data. Movement and performance data remain participant-authored and context-sensitive.

### FELOH Corpus — governance

The cooperative layer requires human decisions about membership, ownership of physical assets, voting, contributions, access, acceptable use, moderation, disputes, financial reporting, maintenance duties, incident response, data stewardship and succession. Technical decentralisation does not settle these questions.

## 5. Cross-cutting decisions

1. Reuse upstream projects and standards; credit creators and preserve licence obligations.
2. Do not conflate mesh, overlay, anonymity, federation, self-hosting and cooperative governance.
3. ActivityPub, Artanis and NGINX are not silently admitted into WARENA's native-first architecture.
4. A USSD response or screenshot is not settlement proof; reconcile against the authoritative payment system.
5. Minimise data and define retention, export, deletion, access and recovery.
6. Specify which functions work without upstream internet, mobile coverage or remote identity.
7. Every service needs an operator, patching, backups, restore tests, abuse handling and succession.
8. This remains RESEARCH: no radio survey, build, benchmark, deployment, provider integration or legal review is claimed.
