## 2. African history and knowledge

### UNESCO General History of Africa
- **Institution:** UNESCO
- **Title:** General History of Africa
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/general-history-africa
- **Type:** Institutional scholarly publication programme and collection portal
- **Accessed:** 2026-11-09
- **Use:** Shared historical and cultural research; especially African historiography, knowledge systems, technologies, institutions, trade, diaspora and regional context.
- **Scope:** UNESCO describes a multi-volume corpus spanning African history from human origins to contemporary Africa and its diasporas. The portal lists volumes I–XI, including methodology and prehistory, ancient civilizations, regional and chronological histories, historiographical revision, diasporas and Global Africa today.
- **Method note:** Start with Volume I for methodology and African prehistory; select later volumes by period, region and research question. Follow the volumes' bibliographies and cited primary evidence rather than treating the portal summary as a substitute for the books.
- **Limitations:** A continent-wide synthesis cannot stand in for local, linguistic, archaeological, oral, environmental or community-specific sources. Preserve regional specificity, contested interpretations, dating uncertainty and scholarly disagreement.
- **Editorial label:** VERIFIED as the collection and programme description; individual historical claims require volume-level citation and corroboration.
- **Rights / reuse:** Link to UNESCO's editions. Do not reproduce full chapters or illustrations without permission or a valid licence.

## 3. Computer science foundations

### Teach Yourself Computer Science
- **Authors / maintainers:** TeachYourselfCS contributors; see the live site for attribution
- **Title:** Teach Yourself Computer Science
- **URL:** https://teachyourselfcs.com/
- **Type:** Curated self-study guide and bibliography
- **Accessed:** 2026-11-09
- **Version note:** The site states that the guide was extensively updated in May 2020. Treat it as a useful structured syllabus, not a continuously current course catalogue.
- **Use:** WARENA / FELOH systems-engineering learning path, curriculum gap analysis and reading-plan scaffolding.
- **Nine subject areas:** Programming; computer architecture; algorithms and data structures; mathematics for computer science; operating systems; computer networking; databases; languages and compilers; distributed systems.
- **Learning guidance:** The site recommends substantial study per subject and pairs texts with lecture series. Its programming recommendation foregrounds SICP and Scheme; this is particularly relevant to the existing Guile/Scheme direction, but does not make Scheme the only language to learn.
- **Limitations:** Resource availability, editions, course versions and links must be rechecked before scheduling. The guide is aimed at people with programming experience who want deeper CS foundations, not absolute beginners.
- **Editorial label:** VERIFIED as the guide's stated syllabus and recommendations; curriculum adoption is PROPOSAL until sequenced and tested against the learner's current level and goals.
- **Rights / reuse:** Link to the original books, lectures and course providers; do not mirror copyrighted course materials.

## 5. Research and learning protocol

1. Record the exact source, access date, version or commit where available.
2. Read the source itself before making a substantive claim; this map is an index, not proof of full-text review.
3. Extract specific claims, passages, chapters, course units or exercises into the relevant research record.
4. Distinguish source statements from our interpretation and implementation decisions.
5. For African history, triangulate with region- and period-specific scholarship, primary evidence and community knowledge where appropriate.
6. For CS, pair reading with problem sets, code, experiments and written explanations; record what was actually completed.
7. For creative practice, document authorship, collaborators, consent, samples, licences, performance context and audience accessibility.
8. Revisit links and versions before relying on them in a release, course plan or public claim.
