### Short description

#NotYetUhuru examines the unfinished work of independence: how political authority is exercised, how public institutions are held accountable, who controls economic resources, and how people participate in shaping their collective future.

### Long description

From Kenya's colonial and constitutional history to continental institutions and diaspora participation, #NotYetUhuru brings historical records, public data, law, political activity and comparative research into one evidence-led programme. It distinguishes documented fact from contested claims and interpretation, and treats governments, opposition formations, civic organisations and external actors under the same standards.

### Editorial voice

- Precise, assertive, curious and historically grounded.
- Use active verbs and concrete institutions.
- Prefer questions that can be investigated over claims that assume an answer.
- Avoid treating Africa as a single political, cultural or economic unit.
- Name countries, peoples, institutions and periods accurately.
- Attribute contested descriptions and political characterisations.
- Avoid inflated claims such as “the people have spoken” unless the evidence and population are specified.

## 2. Visual direction

**Concept:** archival editorial + contemporary civic research publication.

The visual system should feel like a serious research journal, not a candidate poster or generic protest flyer.

| Ink | #101311 | Primary dark field and typography |
| Paper | #F2EFEA | Editorial paper / background |
| Forest | #173B2D | Secondary field |

| White | #FFFFFF | Contrast and negative space |

Kenyan flag colours are a contextual reference, not a substitute for the national flag's official proportions or an assertion that the publication is a state or movement organ.

### Typography

- Display: condensed sans-serif, uppercase, high-impact.
- Editorial headings: restrained serif.
- Body: highly legible sans-serif.
- Metadata: monospaced or compact sans-serif.
- Avoid faux-handwritten protest fonts, excessive distressed textures and dense all-caps body copy.

### Layout system

1. One clear title and one editorial proposition.
2. One dominant image or documentary object.
3. A compact evidence/date strip.
4. A clear separation between research themes and authorship.
5. Consistent footer with series name, edition/date and source status.
6. Use whitespace; do not crowd every research theme onto the cover.

## 3. Image and archive integrity

- Never present AI-generated imagery as an archival photograph, eyewitness image or reconstruction of a specific historical event.
- Label generated cover art **CONCEPT ART / AI-GENERATED** when used in research contexts.
- Preserve supplied photographs without face alteration, identity inference or misleading compositing.
- A supplied image's date, location, photographer, event and original publication must be independently established before factual captioning.
- Obtain permission or confirm licence and rights before republication.
- Record source URL or custodian, creator, date, licence, consent, crop/edit history and caption verification.
- Keep movement logos and official marks out of independent research designs unless permission and affiliation are established.
- Do not imply Linda Mwananchi, ODM, IEBC, the Government of Kenya or any other institution has commissioned or endorsed the work.

The generated concept cover in this conversation is not a historical source. The earlier supplied protest photograph remains provenance-unverified.

## 4. Publication templates

### A. Research cover
Title, thesis line, geography, date, author credit, concept-art or archival-image status.

### B. Evidence card
One claim, one date/period, one source, one evidence label, one limitation.

### C. Timeline
Chronological entries with exact dates, event descriptions, source links and contested interpretations clearly marked.

### D. Comparative brief
Country / institution / legal basis / documented practice / counter-evidence / unresolved question.

### E. Political activity note
Actor / event / date / primary record / independent reporting / response / evidence status.

### F. Data graphic
Metric / unit / period / population / source / method / caveat. Never show a number without its denominator or reporting period where relevant.

## 5. Copy standards

Prefer:
- “The Constitution provides…” over “The Constitution guarantees in practice…”
- “The Treasury reported…” over presenting an official estimate as an independently audited fact.
- “The filing states…” over “the campaign raised…”
- “The index documents…” over implying comprehensive observation.
- “The source alleges…” over adopting an allegation.
- “The proposal would…” over describing an unimplemented proposal as policy in force.

Avoid:
- “The colonial state shall fall” as the default dossier title; it is a separate rhetorical slogan and does not describe the programme's neutral research method.
- Claims that all contemporary institutions are unchanged colonial structures.
- Unqualified claims about foreign control, political motives or electoral outcomes.
- Fabricated historical attributions, quotations, crowd identities or image provenance.
