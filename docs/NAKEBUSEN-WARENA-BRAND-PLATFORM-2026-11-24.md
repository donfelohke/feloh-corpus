## 1. Executive definition

NAKEBUSEN is the primary enterprise and customer-facing identity. WARENA is its distinct technical capability and infrastructure practice, expressed in the endorsement **WARENA POWERED**. The phrase does not make WARENA a parent company, subsidiary, competing shop or separate checkout interface.

GRACE · GRIT · GLORY is the shared philosophy. GENJE ZA WARENA names a creative and enabling relationship: GENJE remains an independent cultural and embodied creative practice. Don Feloh is the human author connecting these practices.

**ONLY NAKEBUSEN TRANSACTS.** Commercial offers, enquiries, availability, final terms and payment instructions remain governed by NAKEBUSEN. An enquiry is not a confirmed booking.

This document defines the shared brand narrative and messaging. It does not replace specialist service, operational, technical, research, rights or privacy records.

## 4. Brand story

### From roots to possibility

NAKEBUSEN is grounded in the belief that beauty, dignity, knowledge and self-determination belong together.

The enterprise began in Kanamkemer, Lodwar, Turkana County, and its loc-care practice now has an operating presence in Eldoret Town, Kenya. Its name is understood by its founder as “Beautiful”; that founder-provided interpretation is part of the brand's identity and should not be presented as a fully verified dictionary definition until supported by appropriate Turkana linguistic evidence.

NAKEBUSEN brings together professional loc care, personal expression, craftsmanship and practical digital services. It values the person, the work, the relationship and the knowledge carried through each practice.

WARENA extends the enterprise's capability into computing and infrastructure: GNU/Linux, free software, networking, reproducibility and locally controllable systems. “WARENA POWERED” communicates that technical relationship without obscuring NAKEBUSEN as the customer-facing enterprise.

GENJE ZA WARENA expresses the creative energy around sound, movement, code and experimentation. It is not a separate commercial unit or a claim that creative culture can be reduced to technology.

The work is connected by one conviction: people should be able to honour where they come from while developing the freedom and capability to shape what comes next.

**Beautiful Roots. Sovereign Systems.**

## 6. Values — GRACE · GRIT · GLORY

### GRACE — NAKEBUSEN

**Beauty · Heritage · Craft · Dignity**

Respect for identity, natural beauty, cultural inheritance, individuality and the dignity of every person. Grace is expressed through attentive service, skilled craft, care, honest communication and respect for the person whose crown is being cared for.

### GRIT — WARENA

**Freedom · Engineering · Resilience · Infrastructure**

Discipline, practical knowledge, technical independence, persistence and the determination to build systems people can understand, maintain, inspect and control. Grit is expressed through reproducibility, documentation, interoperability and evidence-led engineering.

### GLORY — DON FELOH

**Authorship · Expression · Creativity · Becoming**

Human agency, creative excellence, experimentation, accountability and the freedom to define one's own path. Glory is not superiority over others; it is the responsibility to author one's work and contribute meaningfully.

GENJE carries these values into embodied creative expression. IKAI explores reflection and human-computer interaction as research.

## 7. Operating philosophy

1. Roots are foundations, not limitations.
2. Beauty is an expression of identity, not a universal score.
3. Heritage deserves respect, preservation and thoughtful reinterpretation.
4. Craft deserves discipline, knowledge and recognition.
5. Technology should expand human capability, not diminish human agency.
6. Freedom requires understandable, maintainable and recoverable systems.
7. Knowledge should be documented, shared and developed with attribution.
8. African creativity is contemporary, experimental and future-facing.
9. Community is built through relationships, responsibility and contribution.
10. The human remains the author.

**Decision principle:** AI suggests. The human decides.

The practice rejects involuntary profiling, face identification, beauty scoring, surveillance by default, hidden data extraction, unnecessary lock-in and claims of achievement without evidence.

## 8. Audience and relationships

NAKEBUSEN serves people seeking thoughtful loc care and practical digital services. Public communication should remain welcoming and accessible, without assuming that heritage, gender, age, technical expertise or cultural identity determines a person's needs.

Community is not a decorative marketing term. It refers to real relationships, fair dealing, knowledge exchange, responsible service and contribution. Do not publish impact numbers, testimonials, partnerships or community outcomes unless they are documented and approved.

## 11. Naming, etymology and cultural evidence policy

- **NAKEBUSEN:** the founder's stated interpretation is “Beautiful”. Record this as founder-provided meaning pending validation through reliable Turkana language sources and community review. Do not invent a root breakdown, pronunciation or historical derivation.
- **WARENA:** distinguish Kenyan Sheng usage and cultural associations from the owner-defined technical identity. Shared spelling or resonance does not prove historical etymology.
- **GENJE:** treat as a living, context-sensitive Sheng expression. Attribute artist or community explanations to their speakers; do not collapse varied usage into one universal definition.
- **GENJE ZA WARENA:** an authored creative signature expressing a relationship, not a literal corporate or linguistic claim.
- **GRACE · GRIT · GLORY:** an owner-defined philosophical framework, not a traditional African proverb or inherited cultural doctrine unless independently evidenced.

Cultural validation should involve credible language resources and, where appropriate, knowledgeable speakers and practitioners. Record source, date, context, attribution, uncertainty and permission.

## 12. Visual and verbal identity

**Direction:** editorial African-rooted design with restrained dark-terminal depth; craft and human presence remain primary.

**Palette reference:** near-black, ivory, forest green, sage, ochre/gold and terracotta. Use colour semantically and maintain accessible contrast.

**Typography reference:** Cormorant Garamond for editorial display, Inter for body/interface text, JetBrains Mono for technical metadata. Check licensing, local availability and the actual implementation before publication.

**Visual principles:** strong hierarchy, intentional negative space, clear typography, material depth used sparingly, documentary credibility and text-first communication. Avoid generic luxury templates, default Rasta shorthand, decorative cultural motifs without context, gratuitous glitch and unsupported imagery.

## 13. Governance and claim discipline

- NAKEBUSEN owns customer-facing service information, enquiries, availability, final terms and payment instructions.
- WARENA owns its technical capability descriptions and engineering records.
- GENJE owns its creative practice, credits, rights and performance records.
- IKAI owns its research records, participant boundaries and maturity statements.
- FELOH Corpus owns shared identity canon, terminology, evidence and cross-project provenance.
- The founder remains accountable for public claims and approvals.

Use explicit maturity labels: LIVE, PROTOTYPE, RESEARCH, CONCEPT, HISTORICAL, SUPERSEDED or UNKNOWN as appropriate. A document is not a prototype; a commit is not a deployment; a proposal is not an operational capability.

Preserve current service catalogue, contact, location, pricing and payment safeguards in NAKEBUSEN's operational source records. This brand platform does not override those records.
