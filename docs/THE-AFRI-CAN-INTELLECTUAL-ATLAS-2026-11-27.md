# THE AFRI-CAN INTELLECTUAL ATLAS
## African philosophies · Africanisms · Black thought · Pan-African movements · Indigenous knowledge

**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** RESEARCH FRAMEWORK / INITIAL TAXONOMY — not an exhaustive catalogue or a claim that every entry has been independently verified  
**Corpus authority:** FELOH Corpus  
**Human authorship:** Felix Lwova · Don Feloh · @donfelohke  
**Scope:** African-derived concepts, civilisational philosophies, cultural principles, Black intellectual movements, liberation ideologies, ethical systems, identities, organising traditions and comparative worldviews.

> The purpose is not to collect African words as interchangeable slogans. It is to study the languages, peoples, histories, institutions, arguments and practices that give concepts their meaning.

## 1. Taxonomy and category boundaries

The atlas separates related but non-identical fields:

| Category | Working scope | Do not conflate with |
|---|---|---|
| **African philosophy** | Philosophical argument, ethics, metaphysics, epistemology and political thought rooted in African contexts | A single uniform African worldview |
| **Africanisms** | African-derived linguistic, cultural, symbolic and social features, including their transmission and transformation | A synonym for all African philosophy |
| **Africana philosophy** | Philosophical inquiry into African peoples and their global diasporas | Continental African philosophy alone |
| **Afrocentrism** | Africa-centred interpretive and historiographical orientation | Pan-African political organisation |
| **Black philosophy** | Thought grounded in Black historical, social, political and intellectual experience | A single ideology shared by all Black people |
| **Black radical thought** | Traditions analysing and resisting racial domination, colonialism, capitalism and related structures | All Black cultural expression |
| **Pan-Africanism** | Political, cultural and intellectual projects of unity and solidarity across Africa and its diaspora | Every form of African nationalism |
| **Diasporic traditions** | Intellectual, religious, artistic and political formations developed in African-descended communities outside the continent | Direct replicas of continental traditions |
| **Decolonial thought** | Critique and transformation of coloniality in knowledge, power, being and institutions | A synonym for postcolonial literary criticism |
| **Indigenous knowledge systems** | Place- and community-situated knowledge, practices, institutions and transmission | Unowned public-domain material |

“Black”, “African”, “Africana”, “African-descended” and “diasporic” are context-sensitive categories. Use the terms chosen by the people and communities being described, and state the geographical and historical scope.

## 2. Foundational concepts and principles

The following are orientation notes, not dictionary definitions. Orthography, tone marks, dialect, historical period and local usage matter.

| Concept | Linguistic / cultural association | Initial orientation | Research caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sankofa** | Akan, Ghana | Retrieve what is valuable from the past in order to build the future | Study the proverb, symbol forms and Akan context separately |
| **Ubuntu** | Nguni languages; Southern Africa | Relational personhood, humanity and ethical interdependence | Not a universal formula for every African society |
| **Ma’at** | Ancient Egyptian / Kemet | Truth, justice, balance, right order and cosmic-social harmony | Ancient institution, theology and ethics require historical specificity |
| **Harambee** | Kenya; Swahili public/national usage | Pulling together; collective contribution and effort | Trace its political history and changing public uses |
| **Ujamaa** | Swahili; Tanzania | Familyhood, cooperation and Nyerere's political-economic programme | Distinguish ordinary lexical sense from policy and ideology |
| **Umoja** | Swahili; also Kwanzaa principle | Unity | Context determines whether it is a general word or named principle |
| **Kujichagulia** | Swahili; Kwanzaa | Self-determination | Kwanzaa is a modern African-American cultural framework |
| **Ujima** | Swahili; Kwanzaa | Collective work and responsibility | Do not detach from Kwanzaa's modern context |
| **Nia** | Swahili; Kwanzaa | Purpose | General lexical use and named principle are distinct |
| **Kuumba** | Swahili; Kwanzaa | Creativity | Kwanzaa principle includes responsibility to improve community |
| **Imani** | Swahili; Kwanzaa | Faith | Religious and Kwanzaa contexts differ |
| **Aṣẹ / Àṣẹ** | Yoruba | Efficacy, authority, power or capacity through which speech/action can take effect | Yoruba tone, ritual and philosophical context are essential |
| **Ìwà Pẹ̀lẹ́** | Yoruba | Good character; gentle or morally refined conduct | Interpret through Yoruba ethical scholarship, not slogan translation |
| **Omoluabi** | Yoruba | Person of cultivated character, integrity and responsibility | Avoid reducing a complex ethical ideal to “good person” |
| **Seriti** | Sesotho/Setswana and related Southern African contexts | Presence, dignity, moral force or personhood (context-dependent) | Confirm language and local semantic range for each use |
| **Botho** | Setswana/Sesotho contexts | Humanity and ethical personhood | Related to but not automatically identical with Ubuntu |
| **Hunhu** | Shona | Humaneness, character and social responsibility | Preserve Shona context and scholarship |
| **Ntu** | Bantu philosophical discussions | Being / categories of being in particular philosophical interpretations | Do not assert one pan-Bantu doctrine |
| **Amani** | Swahili | Peace | General lexical term; not necessarily a philosophical school |
| **Uhuru** | Swahili | Freedom, independence | Distinguish personal freedom, political independence and liberation movements |
| **Heshima** | Swahili | Respect, honour and dignity | Contextual ethics and ordinary usage need separation |
| **Fihavanana** | Malagasy | Kinship, solidarity and harmonious social relations | Study Malagasy institutions and social history |
| **Fokonolona** | Malagasy | Local community / collective organisation | Historical and contemporary administrative meanings differ |
| **Teranga** | Wolof, Senegal | Hospitality, generosity and social belonging | Avoid turning a living cultural concept into a tourism slogan |
| **Jom** | Wolof | Dignity, courage, honour and self-respect | Validate the precise sense in context |
| **Igwebuike** | Igbo | Strength in community / collective being | Requires Igbo linguistic and philosophical sourcing |
| **Imihigo** | Rwanda | Performance commitments and accountability | Examine modern state practice and historical context |
| **Gacaca** | Rwanda | Community-based justice institutions | Distinguish historical practice from the post-genocide state process |

**Kwanzaa's Nguzo Saba:** Umoja, Kujichagulia, Ujima, Ujamaa, Nia, Kuumba and Imani. Record this as a modern African-American cultural framework drawing on Swahili terms; do not imply that the seven-principle system is an ancient pan-African institution.

## 3. Akan philosophy and Adinkra

Treat Akan philosophy as a living intellectual tradition, not merely a symbol catalogue. Research language, proverbs, moral reasoning, social institutions, cosmology, political history, craft and the contexts in which Adinkra cloth and symbols are made, used and interpreted.

| Concept / symbol | Common orientation | Verification requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Sankofa** | Learning from and retrieving the past | Distinguish bird and heart-form visual traditions; cite Akan sources |
| **Gye Nyame** | The supremacy / incomparable power of God | Document religious context and spelling |
| **Dwennimmen** | Strength with humility | Trace proverb and visual interpretation |
| **Fawohodie** | Independence / freedom | Check spelling and historical use |
| **Nkyinkyim** | Initiative, dynamism, adaptability | Source-specific interpretation |
| **Bi Nka Bi** | Avoid destructive conflict | Record variants and proverb context |
| **Eban** | Protection / security | Verify design and use |
| **Aya** | Endurance and resourcefulness | Distinguish symbol from plant reference and local explanation |
| **Akoma** | Patience, tolerance, goodwill | Record context-specific meanings |
| **Nyansapo** | Wisdom, ingenuity | Include proverb and Akan language evidence |
| **Mpatapo** | Reconciliation and peacemaking | Trace knot symbolism and community use |
| **Epa** | Handcuffs; law / justice in some catalogues | Treat interpretation as source-dependent |
| **Owuo Atwedee** | Mortality; ladder of death | Handle with religious and funerary context |
| **Hwehwemudua** | Examination, quality and authenticity | Confirm spelling and source attribution |

Adinkra symbol meanings vary across published catalogues and community explanations. Record the source, Akan spelling, transliteration, visual form, maker/community attribution, date and use context. Do not reproduce sacred, restricted or commercially controlled designs without checking rights and cultural protocols.

## 4. African civilisational philosophies and indigenous systems

This is a research map, not an assertion that every people has one bounded or internally uniform “philosophy”.

- Ancient Egyptian / Kemetic philosophical, ethical, religious and political thought.
- Akan, Yoruba, Igbo, Edo, Fon and other West African philosophical traditions.
- Dogon cosmological traditions, studied with careful attention to ethnographic history and contested representations.
- Serer cosmology and intellectual heritage.
- Wolof ethical, political, poetic and oral traditions.
- Maasai and wider Maa-speaking knowledge, ethics and institutions.
- Turkana and Ateker intellectual traditions.
- Luo, Luhya, Kikuyu, Kamba, Mijikenda and other Kenyan intellectual and oral traditions.
- Oromo Gadaa institutions, law, political thought and knowledge.
- Somali philosophical, poetic, legal and oral traditions.
- Ethiopian and Eritrean Christian, Islamic, Jewish and philosophical traditions.
- Nubian and Kushite intellectual heritage.
- Amazigh / Berber language, law, philosophy and cultural traditions.
- Zulu, Xhosa, Tswana, Sotho, Shona and other Southern African traditions.
- Malagasy philosophical, ecological and social traditions.
- San and Khoikhoi knowledge systems, with community rights and provenance safeguards.
- African traditional ecological knowledge, agricultural knowledge, healing knowledge and environmental ethics.

Do not assume that an ethnic label maps neatly onto a single philosophy, language, territory or timeless cultural essence. Account for internal diversity, historical change, urban life, religious plurality and contemporary intellectual production.

## 8. Rastafari, Ethiopianism and diasporic spiritual traditions

Research Rastafari as a historically situated Jamaican movement with diverse mansions, practices and interpretations. Relevant terms include **livity**, **I-and-I**, **I-tal**, **Zion**, **Babylon**, **Nyabinghi**, and dread consciousness. Study Bobo Ashanti, Twelve Tribes of Israel and other formations on their own terms. Include Garveyism, Ethiopianism, Haile Selassie I's reception, Ethiopian Orthodox heritage, African diasporic spiritualities and Black liberation theology as related but distinct contexts.

Do not flatten these into generic “African spirituality”. Distinguish theology, ritual, political critique, music, community practice, historical influence and external representation. Attribute teachings to specific communities and sources; respect restricted knowledge and living practitioners.

## 9. Black aesthetics, futurisms and cultural renaissance

Afrofuturism; Africanfuturism; Afro-surrealism; Afro-modernism; Afro-minimalism; Afrocentric design; Black futurism; Black speculative fiction and science fiction; Black digital aesthetics; Black technological imagination; Black sonic philosophy; Black visual culture; artistic resistance; creative sovereignty; African Renaissance aesthetics; Indigenous futurisms; postcolonial futurisms; decolonial design; African digital renaissance.

**Afrofuturism and Africanfuturism are related, not identical.** Use the artist/author's own framing and explain the geographic and diasporic orientation. Do not treat Africanfuturism as a mere subgenre or substitute label for all African speculative work.

Include music, dance, fashion, hair, architecture, film, literature, gaming, code art, electronic music, sound systems and embodied performance as intellectual and aesthetic production—not only as entertainment.

## 10. East African, Kenyan and Turkana research priorities

### 10.1 East African and Kenyan concepts

Harambee; Uhuru; Ujamaa; Umoja; Maendeleo; Heshima; Utu; Imihigo; Gacaca; and concepts from Swahili, Somali, Oromo, Amharic, Maa, Turkana, Dholuo, Gikuyu, Kamba, Luhya, Kalenjin, Mijikenda and other regional languages. For each entry, record exact language, dialect, spelling, pronunciation, grammar, usage, community and source.

Terms such as **Bunengi** (reported Gikuyu association), **Omundu** (Luhya contexts), and **Eniyan / Eniyan** (Yoruba forms) are provisional research leads here, not approved definitions. Confirm the language form and meaning with reliable dictionaries, scholarship and competent speakers before publication.

### 10.2 Turkana intellectual traditions — priority research frontier

Build the research from Turkana language, oral history, community scholarship and named knowledge holders. Initial themes:

- Personhood, community, identity and belonging.
- Eldership, wisdom, authority and intergenerational transmission.
- Age-set organisation, kinship, family and social obligations.
- Cattle, wealth, exchange, livelihood and social relations.
- Land, territory, water, seasons, ecology and mobility.
- Pastoralist ethics, reciprocity, hospitality and mutual aid.
- Conflict, negotiation, reconciliation and customary justice.
- Oral history, memory, genealogy, poetry, song and narrative.
- Spirituality, cosmology, ritual and relationships with place.
- Courage, responsibility, dignity, reputation and honour.
- Adaptation, resilience, innovation and changing livelihoods.
- Contemporary Turkana intellectual life, urban experience and diaspora.

These are **research themes**, not claims that each has one established Turkana philosophical term. Do not invent or reverse-engineer Turkana words to fill taxonomy slots. Record speaker identity only with informed permission; respect community ownership, restrictions, consent, benefit and right of correction.

## 12. Comparative worldviews and methodological safeguards

Comparison should identify a specific question and preserve difference. For example, compare relational personhood across Ubuntu, Botho, Hunhu, Utu and other traditions only after establishing each term's language, community, history and philosophical treatment. Similar translations do not prove shared origin or identical meaning.

For every knowledge entry, capture:

1. Preferred name and variant spellings, including diacritics and tone marks.
2. Original language, language variety and grammatical form.
3. Community, region and relevant social institution.
4. Historical period and contemporary usage.
5. Primary source, scholarly literature and source quality.
6. Literal gloss, contextual meaning and interpretive range.
7. Proverb, oral account, ritual, text or practice where relevant.
8. Who supplied the interpretation and under what authority.
9. Disagreements, contested readings and uncertainty.
10. Rights, consent, restrictions, attribution and benefit-sharing.
11. Current status: attested, corroborated, contested, provisional or unverified.
12. Translation and transliteration decisions.

Never infer an entire culture from one proverb, symbol, interview or online list. Avoid pan-African flattening, romanticisation, essentialism, invented etymologies and the treatment of living knowledge as ownerless material.

## 14. Research programme and deliverables

1. Establish an annotated bibliography spanning African philosophy, Africana philosophy, Black studies, Pan-African history, indigenous knowledge and regional language scholarship.
2. Build a concept register with source-level metadata and evidence status.
3. Create separate Akan/Adinkra, Yoruba, Nguni/Sotho/Shona, Swahili/Kwanzaa, Wolof, Malagasy and East African research clusters.
4. Commission or conduct consent-based Turkana language and oral-knowledge work with competent speakers and community review.
5. Map thinkers, primary texts, movements, institutions, dates and disagreements.
6. Build a visual atlas only after image provenance, cultural rights and symbol context are documented.
7. Develop learning pathways: foundational concepts; African philosophical methods; political thought; Black and diasporic movements; indigenous knowledge; futures and sovereignty.
8. Maintain an errata and correction process. A source discovery may change an entry without erasing its history.
