## 2. Brand architecture and authority

NAKEBUSEN is the enterprise and the primary customer relationship. WARENA is its distinct technical and infrastructure capability. GENJE ZA WARENA is the cultural and creative expression connecting authored sound, movement and computation with technical enablement.

- **NAKEBUSEN:** ROOT — heritage-conscious beauty, loc care, craft, dignity, service and enterprise. The sole customer-facing commercial transaction interface.
- **WARENA:** CAPABILITY — libre-first systems engineering, GNU/Linux, infrastructure, networking, reproducibility and technical enablement.
- **GENJE:** EXPRESSION — Genge-rooted sound, livecoding, audiovisual practice, movement, choreography and creative computation. GENJE ZA WARENA names a creative/enabling relationship, not a subsidiary or product merger.
- **IKAI:** MIRROR — reflective-computing and embodied-interface research; not a salon feature or a deployed product unless a specific record proves that maturity.
- **FELOH Corpus:** shared canon, definitions, governance, evidence, provenance and cross-project navigation.
- **Gallery:** supporting asset provenance, rights, consent and approval.
- **Don Feloh / Felix Lwova / @donfelohke / #HisGenjeness:** founder, author and creative practitioner connecting the work.

**GRACE · GRIT · GLORY is the shared ethos, not three competing organisational identities or a corporate chart.** Any mapping of the pillars to people or practices is an interpretive communication device; it must not override the responsibilities above.

> FELOH generates. NAKEBUSEN grounds. IKAI reflects. WARENA enables. GENJE embodies.

**ONLY NAKEBUSEN TRANSACTS.** WARENA, IKAI and GENJE are not separate booking, checkout or payment interfaces. Customer-facing work must be scoped and agreed through NAKEBUSEN's approved business process.

## 3. Brand essence and strategic platform

**Essence:** Rooted identity, capable hands, self-determined futures.

**Purpose:** To cultivate beauty, dignity, practical capability and self-determination through craft, culture, community and technology.

**Vision:** An Africa in which heritage and innovation reinforce one another, creative enterprise can flourish, and people have the knowledge and agency to shape the systems that govern their lives.

**Mission:** To provide thoughtful loc-care and approved practical services while developing a connected practice of African creativity, technical capability, knowledge-sharing and community-centred systems.

**Positioning:** NAKEBUSEN is a Kenyan, heritage-conscious enterprise connecting professional loc care and practical digital capability. WARENA contributes a libre-first systems-engineering approach; GENJE expresses the wider practice through sound, movement and computation.

**Brand promise:** Honour the individual. Respect the craft. Explain the work. Build with intention. Represent capability honestly.

**Differentiation:** The relationship between physical craft, cultural authorship and practical technical sovereignty—without confusing these distinct activities or overstating their maturity.

## 4. Master narrative

### From roots to possibility

NAKEBUSEN is rooted in the conviction that beauty, dignity, craftsmanship and self-determination belong together.

Its story begins in Lodwar, Turkana County, Kenya, and continues through a practice that values individuality, thoughtful loc care, cultural memory and meaningful relationships. Beauty is not a single imposed standard; it is also care, identity, confidence and the freedom to express oneself.

The enterprise grows through craft and service. It treats practical work as a discipline: listen carefully, understand the person and the requirement, communicate the scope, respect the time and deliver with care.

WARENA extends the same concern for agency into technology. Its libre-first, local-first and inspectable engineering philosophy asks whether people can understand, maintain, recover and govern the systems they depend on. These are design commitments and areas of engineering work; each operational claim must be supported by implementation and test evidence.

GENJE ZA WARENA carries the relationship into cultural expression: Genge, sound, rhythm, movement, livecoding and computational experimentation. It treats culture as living authorship and technology as a medium—not as a substitute for the human creator.

GRACE · GRIT · GLORY names the ethos connecting these activities: respect for people and heritage, discipline in the work, and the freedom to create and become.

NAKEBUSEN honours the roots. WARENA develops capability. GENJE gives the practice movement.

**Beautiful Roots. Sovereign Systems.**

## 5. Values and behavioural standards

### GRACE — dignity in how we serve
- Respect individuality, identity, culture and personal boundaries.
- Treat beauty as plural; do not shame, rank or impose a universal appearance standard.
- Practise care, cleanliness, informed service and honest communication.
- Recognise craft and the people who sustain it.
- Seek consent for photographs, stories and public representation.

### GRIT — discipline in what we build
- Prefer libre software, open standards, interoperability and user control.
- Design for inspectability, maintainability, repair, recovery and offline resilience.
- Document decisions, dependencies, versions, methods and limitations.
- Reuse and credit existing work before recreating it.
- Do not claim a system is secure, reproducible, deployed or sovereign without evidence.

### GLORY — integrity in what we create
- Keep human authorship and creative agency visible.
- Experiment with discipline; credit collaborators and sources.
- Treat African creativity as contemporary, diverse and future-facing.
- Share knowledge responsibly and distinguish interpretation from fact.
- Let the human decide: AI may assist, but does not own the judgement or authorship.

## 6. Brand voice and messaging rules

**Voice:** grounded, assured, warm, precise, culturally situated, technically literate and forward-looking.

**Use:** clear English; Kenyan English and Kiswahili/Sheng where context and audience warrant; concrete verbs; named services; explainable technical language; attributed cultural references.

**Avoid:** generic corporate superlatives, forced slang, exoticising descriptions, pan-African generalisations, unsupported etymologies, empty claims of revolution, and promises of guaranteed outcomes.

**Message order:** people and purpose first; service or capability second; philosophy third; technical detail and research evidence where relevant.

## 7. Approved descriptions — working copy

### About NAKEBUSEN
NAKEBUSEN is a Kenyan enterprise rooted in Turkana heritage and guided by beauty, dignity, craftsmanship and practical enterprise. Its loc-care practice values individuality, thoughtful service and meaningful relationships. WARENA is the technical capability associated with NAKEBUSEN DIGITAL. It explores GNU/Linux, libre software, networking, infrastructure, reproducible environments and local-first systems engineering. Its guiding principle is that technology should be understandable, inspectable, maintainable and governable by the people who depend on it. WARENA records research and engineering maturity honestly; a proposed component is not described as deployed until verified.

### About GENJE ZA WARENA
GENJE ZA WARENA is a creative signature connecting Genge-rooted sound, African sonic identity, rhythm, movement, livecoding and computational experimentation. It explores the meeting of culture, code and embodied practice while keeping the human creator central. It is a cultural and creative expression, not a separate commercial transaction interface or proof of a released technical product.

### Founder biography
**Don Feloh (Felix Lwova) — #HisGenjeness** is a Kenyan founder and creative practitioner connecting beauty craft, systems engineering, music, movement and knowledge work. He founded NAKEBUSEN and develops WARENA as a libre-first systems-engineering practice. His GENJE practice explores Genge, livecoding, choreography and computational expression; IKAI is his research direction in reflective computing. His work is guided by human authorship, cultural continuity, technical freedom and self-determination.

## 10. Naming, heritage and evidence register

The following require careful source work before being presented as externally verified facts:

- **NAKEBUSEN:** the founder understands the name as “Beautiful.” Verify pronunciation, spelling, morphology, language variety, usage and community testimony with competent Turkana-language sources and speakers. Until then, attribute the meaning to the founder's understanding.
- **WARENA:** “Warena Advanced Reproducible Extensible Networked Artefacts” is a founder-defined technical expansion. Any Sheng associations belong in a separately sourced cultural naming dossier; do not present the acronym as historical etymology.
- **GENJE:** record context-sensitive Sheng and music-scene usage with source, speaker/artist attribution, date and context. “The Fire of the Formidable” is a creative interpretation, not a universal translation.
- **Turkana and African heritage:** avoid treating one community as representative of all Africa. Identify the people, place, period, source and limits of each claim.
- **Community and economic impact:** report only activities and outcomes with records, dates and consent.
- **Technical sovereignty:** define the specific control, data, software, hosting, recovery and governance properties achieved; avoid using “sovereign” as an unsupported blanket certification.

### Evidence labels
Use the Corpus evidence vocabulary: VERIFIED, CORROBORATED, DISPUTED, UNVERIFIED, INTERPRETATION, OPINION, TESTIMONY and PROPOSAL. Record a source, date, author/speaker, context and confidence for every material claim.

### Maturity labels
Use LIVE, PROTOTYPE, RESEARCH, CONCEPT, HISTORICAL, SUPERSEDED and UNKNOWN. A document or source file does not prove a release, test, deployment, customer outcome or reproducible build.
