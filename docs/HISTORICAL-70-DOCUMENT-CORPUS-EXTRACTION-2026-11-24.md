## 1. Why this record exists

The historical export contains a large body of authored work, but its size and repeated master-document structure make it easy to confuse repetition with independent evidence. The correct next step is not to publish another undifferentiated master book. It is to identify each source's contribution, preserve its provenance, route its subject to the right specialist repository, and state whether its claims remain current.

The governing architecture is:

**ONE CORPUS → MANY VIEWS → ONE PRACTICE.**

FELOH Corpus owns shared definitions, cross-domain relationships, evidence rules, provenance and decision history. Specialist repositories own detailed domain research and implementation records. The public website is a curated audience-facing view, not the knowledge base.

The current four-practice structure remains:

- **NAKEBUSEN / ROOT:** beauty, loc care, heritage, craft and the only live commercial practice.

## 2. Source provenance and extraction method

The supplied `INDEX.md` describes 70 source files in the export folder. Plain-text and Markdown sources were copied verbatim; HTML was converted with Pandoc; PDF text was extracted with `pdftotext -layout`; DOCX and ODT were converted with Pandoc. The index preserves source filenames so, for example, HTML and PDF editions of similarly named documents remain distinct.

This conversion method creates known limitations:

1. PDF tables, columns, diagrams, page relationships and footnotes may be imperfectly represented in extracted text.
2. HTML/DOCX/ODT conversion may change layout and some structural semantics.
3. A Markdown export is not a substitute for the original binary or rendered source when layout, images, typography or embedded objects carry meaning.
4. Similar titles do not establish identical content, authorship or revision lineage.
5. Word count is inventory metadata, not a measure of evidential quality or originality.

### 3.2 Durable constitutional ideas

The historical sources repeatedly develop a connected set of propositions:

- human authorship and decision authority remain with the person;
- freedom must be supported by architecture, not merely stated as a value;
- local operation and recoverability matter when connectivity is absent;
- network participation should extend a node, not become a prerequisite for its basic usefulness;
- reflection and interpretation should precede automated action;
- technical capability does not confer authority;
- an implementation claim requires observable evidence;
- commercial authority must remain with NAKEBUSEN.

These ideas are now expressed more precisely in the current corpus grammar and technology policy. The older texts should be mined for distinctive arguments, examples and provenance—not copied wholesale into a new constitution.

### 3.4 WARENA: infrastructure proposals need capability-level comparison and test evidence

The historical technical documents span GNU/Linux, system architecture, networking, storage, custom computers, reproducible environments, deployment and recovery. Their main value is a catalogue of problems and candidate approaches.

The extracted Phase 1 checklist is useful for its emphasis on staged implementation and validation. Its proposed targets and procurement assumptions remain proposals unless backed by dated operational records. A checklist is not proof that the listed work happened.

The current WARENA repository already has more specific source records for architecture, reproducibility, desktop policy, networking/federation, distributed storage, cooperative infrastructure and camera/display real-time I/O. Those specialist documents should be extended when new evidence is found; this Corpus record should not fork them into competing technical specifications.

**Research rule:** compare existing standards, upstream projects and proven components before inventing a new layer. Record the problem, workload, constraints, candidate systems, interoperability boundary, licence, maintenance model, failure modes and test results.

### 3.5 IKAI: the mirror is an embodiment, not the whole research category

The Bibliotech v4 source contains an expanded IKAI research corpus. Its contribution is the broad connection between reflective environments, optical systems, displays, sensing, local computation, interaction and applications. The older material should be interpreted alongside the current IKAI research dossier and mirror validation specification.

The current research boundary is narrower and more testable:

- preserve the physical reflection as the primary visual reference;
- make digital information optional, distinguishable and dismissible;
- do not require a camera or sensor for the basic M1 demonstrator;
- treat tracking as a separate opt-in experiment;
- keep core operation local and offline-capable;
- do not identify, rank, diagnose, score or covertly profile people;
- do not infer health, emotion, personality, identity or capability from appearance or movement.

The research question is not simply how to build a smart mirror. It is how an environment can support self-observation, reflection and creation while leaving timing, meaning, memory and action under participant control.

### 3.6 GENJE: movement and music need interoperable layers, not a replacement universal format

The historical Genge, Odi, choreohacking and Laban materials establish a creative-research direction connecting sound, rhythm, movement, code and performance. The expanded Laban feasibility study is a bounded technical proposal, not evidence that a complete Linux-native movement toolchain exists.

Current GENJE research has already advanced the subject through a proposed Movement Intermediate Representation and a movement/music interoperability study. These distinguish authored scores, human interpretation, measured motion, generated sequences, music events and rendered animation.

Established systems such as Labanotation/Kinetography Laban, Motif, BVH, C3D, FBX, OSC and MIDI solve different problems. The proposed Movement IR should act as a semantic and temporal integration contract, not replace these formats. Any adapter must disclose assumptions, uncertainty and transformation loss. A score is not a sensor measurement; a measurement is not an interpretation; generated motion is not an authored cultural account.

Camera, wearable, XR and model-based pathways remain optional research tracks, not prerequisites for the initial text- and score-first work.

## 4. Cross-domain technical synthesis

### 4.1 Node-first, federation-second

A recurring architectural idea across the archive is that a node should remain useful independently while federation adds capabilities. This is the strongest shared engineering thesis to carry forward.

It implies:

- local records and workflows remain usable without internet access;
- network transport is replaceable and optional where the task permits;
- identity and trust boundaries are explicit;
- data export and recovery do not depend on one remote service;
- local failure does not silently destroy the only copy of important records;
- federation must not become an unreviewed authority over local data or human decisions.

### 4.2 Do not collapse networking technologies into one layer

The historical candidate list includes low-power radio, mesh routing, overlay networking, peer-to-peer frameworks and application federation. These are not interchangeable.

- **Physical/link layer:** radio, Ethernet, Wi-Fi and other transports.
- **Local mesh/routing:** forwarding and reachability across local links.
- **Overlay/network identity:** addressing, peer identity and end-to-end paths.
- **Application federation:** exchange of messages, objects or services between independent systems.
- **Data synchronisation:** conflict handling, replication and recovery semantics.

A technology should be selected against a defined workload and layer. GNUnet, Reticulum, Yggdrasil, BATMAN-adv, Meshtastic and ActivityPub should not be compared as if they were competing implementations of the same protocol. Existing WARENA networking and cooperative-infrastructure dossiers own the detailed comparisons.

### 4.3 Local records and transactional state

The older offline-node proposals motivate a local-first records architecture. Files, structured records, databases and indexes need explicit authority boundaries.

The current constitutional direction is: **files are canonical; databases are derived; indexes are disposable.** This is a design rule, not a claim that every existing system already follows it.

SQLite may be suitable for a single-node transactional store, but it must not be treated as a multi-host shared database through a network filesystem. WAL behaviour, SQLite version, checkpointing, backups, power-loss recovery and restore tests must be specified for the actual workload. A successful schema design is not a recovery test.

### 4.4 Real-time bidirectional I/O

The older mirror and camera/display concepts point toward a wider systems problem: synchronised, bidirectional, real-time input/output at scale. The current WARENA and IKAI camera/display dossiers are the right specialist locations for this work.

The shared research questions include:

- capture and display API boundaries;
- buffer ownership and zero-copy or low-copy paths;
- timestamp provenance and clock-domain alignment;
- queue depth, backpressure, latency and jitter;
- frame loss and recovery under load;
- colour, format and resolution conversion;
- privacy and retention boundaries for captured data;
- reproducible measurement and hardware-specific limitations.

No single frame-rate or latency target should be asserted without a named sensor, display, resolution, format, host, driver and measurement method.

## 7. Priority work packages

### WP-01 — Complete source genealogy

For each of the 70 source files:

1. assign a stable source ID;
2. record the original format, export path, word count and source date where known;
3. identify author/editor and source provenance without guessing;
4. compare repeated titles and overlapping passages;
5. identify predecessor, successor, copied sections and conflicting decisions;
6. record extraction quality and any need to inspect the original PDF/HTML/DOCX/ODT;
7. assign a review-depth and disposition label.

### WP-02 — Extract unique contributions

For each document, capture only its distinct contribution:

- central thesis and intended audience;
- definitions and terminology;
- novel or materially useful arguments;
- methods, technical specifications and dependencies;
- cited primary sources and evidence;
- assumptions, forecasts and unverified claims;
- decisions made and decisions still open;
- material superseded by later records;
- destination repository and canonical section.

Do not copy repeated prose merely to make a new document appear comprehensive.

### WP-03 — Build the claims and evidence ledger

A material claim should link to the exact source and section, identify its evidence class, state the scope and limitation, and point to a verification action. A title, bibliography entry or repeated assertion is not proof. Distinguish source-reported facts, independent corroboration, interpretation, proposal and owner-confirmed decision.

### WP-04 — Technical reuse and validation

For each proposed system, identify existing standards, upstream projects, research, hardware and interoperable components. Compare them against the actual use case. Only then identify a gap requiring original engineering.

Every implementation proposal should state:
- target workload and user;
- hardware and software baseline;
- source revisions and licences;
- data and trust boundaries;
- measurable acceptance criteria;
- failure modes and recovery;
- test method and observed result;
- unresolved limitations.

### WP-05 — Reconcile, do not flatten

Keep the following records distinct:
- constitutional decisions;
- specialist research;
- business operating facts;
- technical specifications;
- implementation evidence;
- creative works and performance records;
- historical conversation and authorship archives.

The master index should link these records. It should not become a duplicate of every specialist repository.
