## Purpose

Summarise the regional archaeological and population-genetic picture of Africa from the Late Pleistocene through the African Humid Period and into the later Holocene, as background for the Before Africa programme.

## Core findings

1. **Multiple parallel histories.** Africa’s prehistory is regional, not unitary. Connections and exchanges existed, but so did long periods of relative isolation and independent development.

2. **Green Sahara as one chapter.** The African Humid Period (~14,500–5,000 BP) created lakes, wetlands and grasslands across large parts of what is now desert. Communities fished, hunted, gathered, made pottery and, later in some regions, herded livestock. Aridification after ~5,000 BP prompted adaptation and settlement shift, not simple disappearance.

3. **Cultural diffusion of pastoralism.** The 2025 Takarkori genomes indicate that pastoralism in the central Sahara spread into a deeply divergent, largely isolated North African lineage related to earlier Iberomaurusian (Taforalt) foragers. This supports cultural transmission rather than large-scale demographic replacement in that locality and time slice.

4. **Deep southern African continuity.** Ancient genomes from southern Africa document substantial genetic continuity over at least 9,000 years in sampled populations, together with deep differentiation from other African groups.

5. **Pottery before farming in some regions.** Early Holocene pottery at Ounjougou (Mali) and related Saharan/West African sequences shows ceramic technology associated with hunter-gatherer economies exploiting wild grasses, independent of settled agriculture.

6. **No single continental self-name.** Attested names (Kemet, Kush, Punt, Libu, Afri, Greek Libya/Aithiopes) are regional or external. No primary evidence establishes a universal Indigenous continental name before Roman provincial “Africa.”

## Population continuity types (must be distinguished)

- Biological continuity (genetic relationships)
- Cultural continuity (practices and technologies)
- Linguistic continuity (language transmission)
- Geographical continuity (persistence in or return to landscapes)

These do not necessarily coincide.

## Research priorities

- Expand ancient DNA sampling (Central Africa, additional Saharan and West African Holocene sites).
- Integrate morphometric, isotopic and proteomic data with genomic results.
- Refine archaeolinguistic correlations with tighter chronological control.
- Document local environmental knowledge systems as independent historical records.
- Maintain African scholarly and community leadership in sampling, interpretation and benefit-sharing.

## Maturity

RESEARCH. Findings are subject to revision as new genomes, excavations and linguistic work appear. Two individuals (Takarkori) cannot represent the entire Sahara; sample-size and geographic coverage limitations must be stated whenever these results are cited.
