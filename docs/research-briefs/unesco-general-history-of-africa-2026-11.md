## Canonical institutional entry point

[UNESCO — General History of Africa](https://www.unesco.org/en/general-history-africa)

UNESCO describes the General History of Africa (GHA) as a continent-wide scholarly project launched in 1964. Its current collection page lists eleven volumes and describes collaboration among more than 550 historians and specialists, overseen by an International Scientific Committee with two-thirds African membership.

This is a major institutional corpus and a strong starting point for African-centred historical research. It is not a substitute for specialist scholarship, local archives, community knowledge, oral-history work or critical evaluation of individual chapters and claims.

## Methodological use for BEFORE AFRICA

Prioritise Volume I for historiography, methodology and prehistory. Use the other volumes to identify periods, debates, bibliographies and regional entry points—not as a shortcut to conclusions.

For each topic, preserve distinctions among:
- endonyms, exonyms, ethnonyms, place names and political labels;
- archaeological evidence, inscriptions, manuscripts, oral traditions and later written accounts;
- primary sources, scholarly interpretation, institutional synthesis and popular retelling;
- regional histories and continent-wide comparative claims;
- documented historical usage and contemporary identity or authorial interpretation.

The dossier's existing caution remains in force: do not present a universal indigenous name for the continent, a single African worldview, or a single political system without specific evidence. A continental synthesis does not erase local histories or differences among communities.

## East African and Turkana research pathway

The GHA provides a broad continental frame. It does not by itself settle detailed histories of Turkana, Ateker, pastoral institutions, language, local knowledge or northern Kenyan historical geography.

For those questions, add specific linguistic, archaeological, ethnographic, archival and oral-history sources. Record speaker/community context, location, date, language variety, consent and custodianship. Do not infer a Turkana term or tradition from a general African source.
