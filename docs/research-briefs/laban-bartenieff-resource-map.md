## Research question

Which primary institutional resources can anchor further study of Laban Movement Analysis (LMA), Bartenieff Fundamentals (BF), embodied practice, and movement-related creative technology?

## Findings supported by the sources reviewed

### Finding 1 — LIMS identifies several research-resource pathways

**Label:** VERIFIED — institutional research page inspected.

The Laban/Bartenieff Institute of Movement Studies (LIMS) identifies the Journal of Laban Bartenieff Movement Studies, conferences and symposia, research partnerships, archives, online exhibits, bibliographies, and other study resources. Its page also links to collections at the University of Maryland and describes research projects involving movement and digital tools.

**Source:** [LIMS — Research](https://labaninstitute.org/research/)  
**Relevant page sections:** “LIMS Research,” “Journal of Laban Bartenieff Movement Studies,” “Library & Archives,” and “Research Initiatives and News.”

**Use:** Treat the page as a discovery map. Follow the links to individual papers, archives, projects, and datasets before using them to support a substantive claim.

### Finding 2 — LIMS describes LMA as a movement vocabulary and analytical framework

**Label:** VERIFIED — institutional description; not an independent validation of every application.

LIMS' education page describes Laban Movement Analysis as a vocabulary and analytical framework for identifying patterns and changes in movement. It describes Bartenieff Fundamentals as principles and a methodology developed by Irmgard Bartenieff, who studied with Rudolf Laban, for observing and analysing movement.

**Source:** [LIMS — Education](https://labaninstitute.org/education/)  
**Relevant page sections:** “Introduction to Laban Movement Analysis” and “Introduction to Bartenieff Fundamentals.”

**Limit:** These are the institute's own descriptions of its field and programmes. Claims about validity, reliability, particular applications, or measurable outcomes require individual publications and methods to be reviewed.

## Application to GENJE and IKAI

### GENJE — creative practice

Potential research paths include movement notation/description, choreography, rehearsal and performance documentation, embodied creative practice, and the technical documentation of interactive performance. For each project, record the source, author, method, context, version, and limits. Keep a creative interpretation distinct from a research finding.

### IKAI — reflective-compute research

LMA/BF may be relevant as scholarly and practice-based contexts for thinking about movement, but they must not be used as a shortcut to infer a person's health, emotion, personality, identity, intent, or capability from sensor data. Any proposed digital experiment requires its own research question, data-flow map, participant controls, evaluation plan, and explicit limitations.

A framework for describing movement does not automatically justify automated judgement of people. A demonstration is not validation, and a method's use in one field does not prove suitability for a different technical application.
