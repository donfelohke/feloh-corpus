# UI/UX Operating Standard, Design Trends and Brand Case Studies

**Status:** RESEARCH / RECOMMENDATIONS — not a completed local usability study  
**Review date:** 2026-10-26  
**Canonical home:** FELOH Sovereign Corpus  
**Applies to:** NAKEBUSEN Locs • Digital, WARENA, public web properties and relevant ecosystem interfaces  
**Operating principle:** Global standards; Kenyan context; Eldoret realities; evidence from actual users.

## 1. Purpose and decision

This document turns the current UI/UX research into a reusable operating procedure. It is a decision aid, not proof that the recommendations have been implemented or that local users have validated them.

**Recommended direction:** use an editorial, craft-led foundation across the public brand; make the NAKEBUSEN Locs journey service-first and conversion-clear; make NAKEBUSEN Digital / WARENA practical, scope-led and evidence-oriented. Keep research, GENJE and IKAI as distinct supporting domains rather than competing with the two customer routes.

### Evidence labels

Use these labels in all design records:

- **SOURCE FACT** — directly stated by a cited source.
- **VENDOR-REPORTED RESULT** — a case-study vendor's reported outcome; not independently verified here.
- **LOCAL LISTING** — a business or service listing; not an audit of its website, service quality or conversion.
- **INTERPRETATION** — what the evidence may mean for this project.
- **RECOMMENDATION** — a proposed change to test.
- **UNKNOWN** — requires observation, owner confirmation or user research.

Never convert a vendor case study, design trend article or local directory listing into a claim about measured results at NAKEBUSEN.

## 2. Benchmark case studies

### 2.1 Global: Patagonia — information hierarchy and product discovery

**Source:** [BASIC — Patagonia ecommerce website case study](https://www.basicagency.com/case-studies/patagonia-ecommerce-website); [Chelsea Lensing — navigation redesign](https://www.chelsealensing.design/case-studies/navigation-redesign).

**Reported design lesson:** help people find the right thing with clear hierarchy, useful categorisation and navigation that behaves like a knowledgeable guide. A coherent design system supports consistency across product and content surfaces.

**Transfer to NAKEBUSEN:** organise the site around the two real jobs visitors arrive to do: book/enquire about loc care, or enquire about practical technology services. Put the service, starting price, location and next action before brand mythology or long founder biography. Make every route understandable without requiring visitors to decode internal acronyms.

**Do not copy blindly:** a large outdoor-commerce catalogue has more products, logistics and filters than this compact service website. The relevant lesson is discoverability, not catalogue complexity.

### 2.2 Global: Apple checkout — reduce steps and preserve clarity

**Source:** [Isaiah Andrew — Apple checkout redesign case study](https://isaiahandrew.com/apple-checkout-redesign).

This is a designer-published redesign case study, not an official Apple performance report. Treat any outcome claims on the page as the author's reported findings, not independently verified benchmark data.

**Transfer:** reduce unnecessary decisions, explain each step, keep errors recoverable, and make the next action obvious. For NAKEBUSEN, do not imitate a checkout: the actual process is an enquiry followed by direct confirmation of scope, availability, timing and price. Never imply that a button has booked an appointment or accepted payment.

### 2.3 Kenya: Marini Naturals — hair-care information architecture

**Source:** [Marini Naturals](https://www.marininaturals.com/) and [product collections](https://www.marininaturals.com/collections).

The public product taxonomy provides a useful regional benchmark for organising hair-care choices and protective-style needs. This is a category/content benchmark, not a claim that the brands share the same business model or customer outcomes.

**Transfer:** use language customers recognise, explain service suitability, distinguish loc-care needs, and avoid presenting all hair services as interchangeable. Keep NAKEBUSEN's advertised service scope accurate; do not add braiding simply because it appears in the broader market.

### 2.4 Kenya: Safaricom Baze — multi-audience discovery

**Source:** [Big Bold Red — Safaricom Baze UX case study](https://www.bigboldred.agency/case-studies/safaricom-baze-scalable-ux-for-digital-lifestyle-ecosystem/).

The case study discusses discovery and organisation across different content types and audiences. It is an agency case study, so separate its described design approach from independently measured impact.

**Transfer:** a site with several domains must orient different visitors without flattening all needs into one generic page. NAKEBUSEN Locs and Digital should be distinct paths with clear labels and purpose-specific enquiry actions.

### 2.5 Kenya: digital finance and trust

**Sources:** [M-PESA: branding, channel management and pricing](https://www.findevgateway.org/case-study/2010/01/three-keys-m-pesas-success-branding-channel-management-and-pricing); [user involvement in mobile-money design research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/isd2.12180).

These sources support examining trust, clear channels, pricing communication and task usability in local digital services. They do not establish that a beauty enquiry journey should copy a financial transaction flow.

**Transfer:** state starting prices and what affects the final quote; use familiar contact channels; keep the contact path transparent; minimise the information a customer must provide before receiving a human response. Avoid collecting sensitive information without a specific need.

### 2.6 Eldoret / Uasin Gishu — competitor audit status

Initial market-research leads include Alif Locs, Flo Sisterlocks and ADECARGO Dreadlocks for loc-related services, plus local digital providers. These names came from business listings and are **leads to investigate**, not completed digital UX case studies.

For each competitor, a future audit must capture a dated public URL or public profile, screenshots or notes of the actual journey, service and price visibility, contact handoff, mobile behaviour, accessibility observations and evidence limitations. Do not infer that a business has a good or poor website from a directory listing, rating or social follower count alone. Do not copy competitor text or images.

## 3. Design trends: adopt, test or defer

| Pattern | Decision | Reason and guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile-first responsive layouts | ADOPT | Core discovery and contact must work on narrow screens; test 320, 360, 390, 430 CSS px and desktop. |
| Clear editorial typography and generous spacing | ADOPT | Supports craft, readability and the text-first brand. Respect line length and zoom. |
| Design tokens and reusable components | ADOPT | Reduces drift in colour, spacing, focus, buttons and price rows. Keep the system small. |
| Accessible contrast, keyboard support and reduced motion | REQUIRED | Baseline quality, not a visual trend. |
| Purposeful micro-interactions | TEST | Motion must communicate state, remain brief, and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. |
| Human texture / expressive visual identity | TEST | Use typography, language and craft-led copy first; no unapproved imagery under the current text-only web policy. |
| Glassmorphism and decorative blur | DEFER | Can weaken contrast, legibility and performance; use only if a tested need outweighs costs. |
| Animated hero/video background | REJECT FOR CURRENT SITE | Adds weight and distraction, conflicts with the text-only page rule. |
| AI-personalised UX | DEFER | No demonstrated need; introduces privacy, explainability and maintenance costs. |
| Floating booking widget | TEST ONLY | Could obscure content and controls on small screens. A clear WhatsApp enquiry CTA is the current baseline. |
| Social proof / testimonials | REQUIRE EVIDENCE | Publish only genuine, authorised and traceable endorsements. No invented reviews or client claims. |

Trend context: [Nielsen Norman Group — State of UX 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/); [zeroheight — 2026 Design Systems Report](https://report.zeroheight.com/2026); [Designity — UI/UX design trends](https://www.designity.com/blog/ui-ux-design-trends); [GMI Software — mobile app design trends](https://gmi.software/blog/mobile-app-design-trends). Trend articles are directional signals, not substitutes for user evidence.

## 4. Shared design principles

1. **Orient first.** Visitors should understand the brand, offer, location/context and next step within the first screen.
2. **Make two primary journeys obvious.** Locs and Digital must not compete in one undifferentiated CTA.
3. **Put operational facts before lore.** Service scope, starting price, location and enquiry expectations are high-value information.
4. **One action, one meaning.** “Enquire on WhatsApp” is not “Book now” unless the system actually confirms a booking.
5. **Mobile is a full experience.** No clipped controls, horizontal page overflow, unreadable prices or focus hidden behind sticky navigation.
6. **Accessible by default.** Semantic headings, meaningful link text, visible focus, keyboard operation, sufficient contrast, correct language metadata and reduced-motion support.
7. **Text-first and offline-friendly.** Static HTML/CSS and Markdown by default; no tracking, external fonts, required CDN or graphics on the public website unless a separately reviewed decision changes the policy.
8. **Truthful content.** Prices are starting prices; confirm availability, scope and final quote directly. Never invent reviews, credentials, outcomes or portfolio proof.
9. **Privacy by minimisation.** Do not collect more than needed to respond. Do not add analytics or profiling without an explicit, reviewed decision.
10. **Evidence before status claims.** A passing static test is not a browser test; a merged PR is not proof of a live deployment; a concept is not a product.

## 5. UI/UX SOP — from question to release

### Stage 0 — Define the problem

Create a short design brief containing:
- audience and primary task;
- current journey and friction hypothesis;
- intended outcome and measurable success criterion;
- constraints (device, connectivity, language, privacy, brand, content);
- what is in scope and explicitly out of scope;
- owner and review date.

Do not begin with a visual trend. Begin with a user problem.

### Stage 1 — Research and baseline

- Inspect the current repository, open PRs and existing design standards before editing.
- Capture the existing journey at relevant mobile and desktop widths.
- Review direct competitors using dated, public evidence.
- Talk to representative users where possible; record task, observation and consent, not unnecessary personal data.
- Keep source URLs, access date, direct observation, interpretation and confidence separate.
- Record unanswered questions and avoid invented personas or sample sizes.

### Stage 2 — Information architecture and journey

- Name the primary user tasks in customer language.
- Draw the shortest honest path from entry to completion.
- Identify required information, trust cues, error states and the human handoff.
- Include no-result, unavailable, cancellation, retry and contact-failure states where relevant.
- For service enquiries, define what is confirmed by the interface and what remains for a human to confirm.

### Stage 3 — Design system and prototype

Define only the tokens and components the product needs:
- colour roles, typography, spacing, content width and borders;
- buttons, links, navigation, forms, price rows and feedback states;
- hover, focus, active, disabled and error states;
- responsive breakpoints based on content failure, not device marketing labels;
- English/Kiswahili behaviour and document-language updates;
- theme persistence independent of language preference;
- reduced-motion and high-zoom behaviour.

Prototype the structure and copy before polishing decoration. Use realistic prices, labels and long text to expose layout failures.

### Stage 4 — Usability and accessibility checks

Run task-based tests with representative users when feasible. Ask them to complete a task without coaching; observe errors and hesitation. Report the sample and limits honestly.

Minimum manual checks:
- 320 / 360 / 390 / 430 CSS px and a desktop viewport;
- 200% zoom or equivalent text enlargement;
- keyboard-only traversal and visible focus;
- light and dark themes, system preference and persistence;
- language toggle and correct document `lang`;
- link destinations, WhatsApp handoff and phone/email actions;
- contrast, heading order, link names and touch target spacing;
- no horizontal page overflow, hidden content or sticky-header collisions;
- reduced-motion preference;
- browser console and network failures.

Automated linting is a useful filter, not proof of accessibility or usability.

### Stage 5 — Implement and review

- Inspect branch state and related PRs first.
- Use a feature branch and a focused PR; avoid overlapping changes to the same page without reconciliation.
- Add regression tests for the discovered issue, not merely a broad snapshot.
- Record files changed, rationale, source evidence, test output and known gaps.
- Keep code changes separate from content decisions when they can be reviewed independently.

### Stage 6 — Release and observe

- Merge only after review and required checks pass.
- Verify the deployment run and published URL.
- Test the live site on an actual mobile browser and desktop; record date, commit and results.
- Track task success, failed handoffs, repeated customer questions and content corrections without collecting unnecessary personal data.
- Revisit the design when evidence changes; do not report improvements without a baseline and post-change evidence.

## 7. Measures and decision gates

Choose a baseline before implementation. Candidate measures:
- task completion rate for “find the loc service and starting price”;
- task completion rate for “find the correct Digital enquiry path”;
- time and number of missteps to locate contact details;
- successful WhatsApp handoff on a real device;
- rate of repeated questions caused by unclear copy;
- accessibility defects by severity;
- page weight and failed external requests (target: none required by design);
- mobile overflow and keyboard-focus defects.

Do not fabricate numeric targets before baseline. Set targets after initial observation and state the sample size.

**Release gate:** no merge recommendation based solely on trend fit or automated checks. Require source review, successful automated checks, human content review, and browser evidence appropriate to the change. If browser QA is unavailable, label it outstanding.

## 9. Governance and ownership

- This document is the cross-domain SOP and benchmark register.
- NAKEBUSEN owns the operational service journey and customer-facing copy.
- WARENA owns technical-service discovery, scope, delivery evidence and handover.
- The website repository owns HTML/CSS/JS implementation and browser/deployment evidence.
- Gallery owns asset permissions, curation and approved visual materials.
- The FELOH Corpus records shared decisions, source provenance and status changes.

**Status rule:** research → recommendation → prototype → implementation → automated test → browser-verified → deployed. Advance status only when evidence exists.
