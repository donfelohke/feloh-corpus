# THOUGHT & REFLECTION — Interdisciplinary Research Agenda, 2026–2028

**Status:** PROPOSAL · 22 November 2026  
**Scope:** All eight programmes  
**Approach:** Interdisciplinary; African-centred in questions; critical and comparative in method  
**First flagship:** African consciousness and sovereignty  
**Canonical home:** FELOH Corpus

## 1. Research purpose

Build a sustained body of inquiry into African consciousness, freedom, knowledge, institutions, technology, embodiment, existence and cultural production. Investigate how ideas relate to historical structures, material conditions and lived experience.

The programme's central working proposition is that meaningful freedom and collective self-determination may depend on the interaction of political authority, economic capability, knowledge systems, culture, infrastructure and accountable institutions. This is a **PROPOSAL / HYPOTHESIS** to examine, not a conclusion to presume.

The work will distinguish:
- VERIFIED or CORROBORATED findings supported by inspectable sources;
- DISPUTED claims where credible evidence or interpretations conflict;
- INTERPRETATION drawn by the author from evidence;
- OPINION attributed to its author;
- TESTIMONY preserved with context and consent;
- UNVERIFIED claims and material unknowns;
- PROPOSAL and HYPOTHESIS as work not yet established.

## 2. Research programme portfolio

### 01 — African consciousness, identity and sovereignty

**Core question:** What political, economic, intellectual, cultural, technological and local conditions enable African peoples to exercise meaningful self-determination?

Work areas:
- Colonialism, liberation, independence and postcolonial state formation.
- State sovereignty, popular sovereignty, legitimacy and democratic participation.
- Economic autonomy, ownership, productive capacity, trade, finance, resources and debt.
- Intellectual autonomy, education, language, research institutions and knowledge production.
- Cultural identity, heritage, representation and cultural rights.
- Technology, communications, data, infrastructure and technical capability.
- Continental cooperation, regional integration and collective bargaining.
- Community rights, land, livelihoods and local participation.

**Contribution sought:** a critical, multidimensional framework that distinguishes formal independence from the conditions and practices through which people exercise agency.

### 02 — Freedom, dignity and liberation

**Core question:** What makes freedom substantive rather than merely formal?

Work areas:
- Freedom as non-domination, self-determination and practical capability.
- Individual liberty and collective agency.
- Rights, duties, responsibility and dignity.
- Political, economic, cultural and psychological dimensions of liberation.
- Institutions and material conditions required for human agency.

**Contribution sought:** a philosophical account of freedom that can be tested against institutional and lived conditions.

### 03 — Knowledge, truth and epistemic authority

**Core question:** Who produces, validates, preserves, controls and benefits from knowledge?

Work areas:
- African philosophical traditions and indigenous knowledge.
- Oral knowledge, language, testimony, memory and transmission.
- Colonial knowledge systems and conceptual decolonisation.
- Scientific institutions, research funding, archives and knowledge ownership.
- Evidence, uncertainty, interpretation and intellectual accountability.

**Contribution sought:** a framework for epistemic authority that respects critical reasoning, plural traditions and evidence standards.

### 04 — Institutions, political economy and collective agency

**Core question:** How can people exercise meaningful influence over the material conditions of their lives?

Work areas:
- State capacity, public institutions and democratic accountability.
- Ownership, production, distribution, labour and economic justice.
- Cooperatives, commons and alternative institutional arrangements.
- Natural resources, value addition, industrialisation and trade.
- Public goods, infrastructure and collective capabilities.

**Contribution sought:** an evidence-led political-economic account of collective agency and institutional design.

### 05 — Technological freedom and infrastructure

**Core question:** Who owns, operates, maintains and governs the technical systems on which people depend?

Work areas:
- Digital and computational sovereignty.
- Free software, reproducible systems and technical autonomy.
- Communications, networks, distributed systems and real-time I/O.
- Data ownership, privacy, surveillance and platform power.
- Hardware supply chains, repairability, skills and local technical capacity.

**Contribution sought:** a framework for technological autonomy that distinguishes interdependence from structural dependency and isolation.

### 06 — Beauty, embodiment and human expression

**Core question:** How are identity, dignity and agency lived and negotiated through the body, beauty and practices of care?

Work areas:
- Natural hair, locs and African identity.
- Beauty practices, cultural meaning, dignity and self-determination.
- Embodied knowledge, movement and choreography.
- Political economy of beauty, care and labour.
- The body as a site of memory and expression.

**Contribution sought:** an interdisciplinary account of embodied agency grounded in cultural and lived evidence.

### 07 — Existence, home, memory and becoming

**Core question:** How do place, memory, belonging and transformation shape human existence?

Work areas:
- Home as place, relationship, belonging and condition of being.
- Personal memory and collective historical memory.
- Identity, continuity, displacement and transformation.
- Place, time, presence and becoming.
- Inner life and the environments through which people live.

**Contribution sought:** a philosophy of home and becoming attentive to lived experience, language and place.

### 08 — Art, music, movement and cultural production

**Core question:** How is culture created, transmitted, attributed, owned and transformed?

Work areas:
- Genge and contemporary African musical forms.
- Livecoding and algorithmic cultural production.
- Dance, choreography, movement and embodied intelligence.
- Heritage, contemporary innovation and cultural sovereignty.
- Creative authorship, influence, attribution and ownership.

**Contribution sought:** an account of cultural agency that connects creative practice, technology, movement and historical continuity without collapsing distinct traditions.

## 3. Cross-cutting analytical questions

Every programme should consider:
1. Power and agency — who can act, decide, define and refuse?
2. Coloniality and modernity — which inherited structures remain operative, and how can that be demonstrated?
3. Language and meaning — whose concepts and classifications organise the inquiry?
4. Ownership and commons — who controls assets, knowledge, infrastructure and value?
5. Memory and history — what is preserved, omitted, contested or inaccessible?
6. Ethics and responsibility — who bears risks and receives benefits?
7. Interdependence — which relationships create reciprocal capability, and which create asymmetric dependence?
8. Gender, class, age, disability and local difference — whose experiences are hidden by general claims?

## 4. Prioritisation

The programme has broad scope but must be executed in a sequence.

| Priority | Programme | Reason for sequence |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | African consciousness, identity and sovereignty | Flagship; connects political, economic, intellectual, cultural and technical agency |
| 2 | Freedom, dignity and liberation | Clarifies the normative meaning of agency and freedom |
| 3 | Knowledge, truth and epistemic authority | Establishes how claims and knowledge systems are evaluated |
| 4 | Institutions and political economy | Tests the material and institutional conditions of agency |
| 5 | Technological freedom and infrastructure | Extends sovereignty into technical systems and capabilities |
| 6 | Existence, home, memory and becoming | Deepens the programme's account of identity, belonging and lived experience |
| 7 | Beauty, embodiment and human expression | Investigates the embodied and cultural dimensions of dignity |
| 8 | Art, music, movement and cultural production | Connects cultural production, authorship and embodied practice |

Priority is an execution order, not a ranking of intellectual or cultural value.

## 7. Research completion and publication

Use the existing Corpus standards:
- Evidence labels: VERIFIED, CORROBORATED, DISPUTED, UNVERIFIED, INTERPRETATION, OPINION, TESTIMONY, PROPOSAL.
- Maturity labels: LIVE, PROTOTYPE, RESEARCH, CONCEPT, HISTORICAL, SUPERSEDED, UNKNOWN.
- Preserve source, claim, method, limitation, rights, consent and revision data.
- A research plan is not completed research; a public essay is not automatically a peer-reviewed study.

The publication register is maintained separately in [PROPOSED-PUBLICATION-REGISTER.md](./PROPOSED-PUBLICATION-REGISTER.md).

## 8. Review

Review this agenda at least annually and whenever new evidence materially changes the programme. Revisions must preserve the previous rationale and record why priorities, scope or definitions changed.
