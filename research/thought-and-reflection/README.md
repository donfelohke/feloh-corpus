## 1. Purpose

THOUGHT & REFLECTION is the editorial programme for sustained inquiry into worldview, philosophy, thought, opinion, reflection and intellectual influences. Its purpose is to develop a coherent, source-grounded body of work across African consciousness, freedom, knowledge, institutions, technology, embodiment, existence and cultural production.

The programme is interdisciplinary: it draws on philosophy, history, law, political economy, anthropology, linguistics, science and technology studies, aesthetics, musicology and other relevant disciplines. It is African-centred in the questions it asks, while remaining critical, comparative and open to evidence from any intellectual tradition.

**Working proposition — PROPOSAL, not an established conclusion:** human freedom and collective sovereignty may depend not only on legal or political status but also on the knowledge, institutions, material capabilities, cultural conditions and technical systems through which people exercise agency. The proposition must be tested, revised or rejected where evidence warrants.

## 4. Editorial categories and research programmes are different

The website's six editorial categories describe how readers encounter a work:

- Worldview
- Philosophy
- Thought & Ideas
- Opinion
- Reflection
- Influences

The eight research programmes describe how inquiry is organised. One research programme may produce multiple kinds of publication. A sovereignty investigation, for example, may yield a research dossier, a philosophical argument, a reflective essay and an attributed opinion piece. Each must be labelled according to what it actually is.

## 5. Intellectual and evidence principles

1. Preserve human authorship. The author owns positions and decisions; research assistance does not become authorship.
2. Study existing work before proposing novelty. Reuse, adapt, interoperate and credit where appropriate.
3. Prefer primary sources for legal, historical and institutional claims.
4. Engage African scholarship and intellectual traditions without treating Africa as culturally or philosophically uniform.
5. Compare positions and include credible counterarguments.
6. Separate facts, interpretations, opinions, hypotheses, proposals and testimony using the Corpus evidence labels.
7. Preserve uncertainty, contradictions, limitations and revision history.
8. Respect privacy, rights, consent and community protocols, especially for oral, indigenous, sacred or restricted knowledge.
9. Do not present a research agenda, conceptual model or proposed publication as completed research.
10. A public website entry is a selected view; the authoritative research record remains in this Corpus.

## 6. Status vocabulary

Use the existing Corpus claim labels and maturity labels. For editorial workflow, use these working descriptors where helpful:

- QUESTION — an open question.
- EXPLORATION — preliminary inquiry.
- WORKING THOUGHT — a developing idea or hypothesis.
- REFLECTION — considered examination.
- POSITION — an explicitly held view.
- ARGUMENT — a developed line of reasoning.
- RESEARCH — evidence-led investigation.
- PROPOSAL — a recommended future course.
- REVISED — materially updated.
- RETRACTED — withdrawn.

These descriptors are not quality rankings and do not replace the mandatory evidence labels or maturity labels.

## 8. Canonical record and change control

- This repository is the source of record for programme definitions, source annotations, claim registers and revisions.
- The website may publish selected, approved summaries and links; it must not become a competing research archive.
- Specialist repositories retain their detailed applied research and implementation records.
- Substantive changes use a focused branch and pull request.
- Every publication must identify its canonical Corpus path and status.
- Do not assign publication dates or mark works complete before the work exists and passes editorial review.
