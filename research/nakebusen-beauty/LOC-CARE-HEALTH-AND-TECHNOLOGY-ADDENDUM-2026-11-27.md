## 2. Hair and scalp care: bounded evidence

The American Academy of Dermatology identifies traction alopecia as hair loss caused by repeated or sustained pulling. Its public guidance lists locs among styles that can contribute when they are too tight. The relevant operational implication is not that locs are inherently harmful: risk is associated with tension, duration, individual susceptibility and styling practice.

**Practice implications for a professional loc-care setting:**
- Ask the client to report pain, pulling, headache, tenderness or discomfort during styling; do not normalise pain as a necessary part of a service.
- Avoid excessive tension at the hairline and around vulnerable areas.
- Do not promise that a retie, product or styling method prevents or treats hair loss.
- Encourage clients to seek qualified clinical assessment for persistent pain, inflammation, sores, scaling, sudden or unexplained shedding, or suspected alopecia.
- Treat aftercare as general craft guidance, not diagnosis or treatment.
- Record only service-relevant information and only where the client has agreed to its collection.

This is a risk-awareness baseline, not a clinical standard or a substitute for individual medical advice.

## 3. Black hair, identity and cultural representation

The 2022 review “Historicizing Black Hair Politics” analyses hair as a site of political control, racialisation and resistance in the Americas. It is a useful scholarly framework for understanding why Black hair can carry social and political meaning. Its geographic scope is not a complete account of African hair histories and should not be transferred wholesale to Kenya.

For NAKEBUSEN:
- Treat each client's reasons for wearing locs as their own.
- Do not claim that all locs have one origin, one spiritual meaning or one political meaning.
- Research specific African communities, periods and practices using community sources, primary material and specialist scholarship.
- Distinguish founder-provided naming context, client testimony, historical evidence and brand interpretation.
- Obtain explicit, informed permission before publishing client photographs, testimonials or identifiable stories.

## 4. Salon technology: workflow before automation

The existing technology dossier correctly recommends beginning with a dependable, human-confirmed enquiry workflow rather than building a custom salon platform prematurely.

### Minimum useful workflow

1. Publish the approved service catalogue and fixed prices.
2. Receive an enquiry through the approved customer channel.
3. Check actual practitioner availability.
4. Confirm the appointment explicitly with the client.
5. Record only necessary appointment information in a private register.
6. Mark the appointment requested, confirmed, completed, cancelled or no-show.
7. Track aggregate business measures without exposing customer records.
8. Maintain export, backup and restore procedures.

An enquiry is not a confirmed booking. A WhatsApp link is not a booking engine.

### Automation candidates — research only

| Capability | Initial approach | Gate before automation |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiry intake | Human-managed WhatsApp / phone | Confirm channel, response ownership and consent |
| Appointment register | Small private register | Access control, export, backup and retention |
| Reminders | Optional, later | Consent, channel costs, delivery failures and opt-out |
| Inventory | Simple stock log if needed | Demonstrated recurring operational need |
| Customer analytics | Aggregate counts | Data minimisation and no unnecessary profiling |
| Visualisation | IKAI research only | Explicit participant control, privacy and validated hardware |

Do not put client details, private photographs, appointment records, credentials or production exports in a public Git repository.

## 5. Kenya market context: what official statistics do and do not show

KNBS's 2026 Economic Survey reports an estimated 18.1061 million people engaged in Kenya's informal sector in 2025, a 4.1% increase. Its wholesale and retail trade, hotels and restaurants grouping accounts for 10.7415 million. These are broad national activity categories.

**These figures are not a count of beauty workers, loc practitioners, salon customers or addressable loc-care revenue.** They cannot be used as NAKEBUSEN TAM, SAM or SOM without additional local evidence.

The KNBS 2026 Integrated Labour Force Survey announcement describes a nationwide survey covering informal employment and digital-platform employment. Treat it as a future source to revisit when published results and tables are available; do not cite the announcement as if it contained final estimates.

### Eldoret validation plan

Build a local evidence base before making market-size claims:
- Map comparable loc-care providers and publicly listed offers; date every observation.
- Conduct consent-based interviews with current, former and prospective clients.
- Ask about maintenance intervals, service choice, trust, travel, appointment friction and willingness to pay.
- Record service duration, consumables, overhead, cancellations and repeat visits.
- Separate observed transactions from stated willingness to pay.
- Use a transparent sample description; do not present a convenience sample as representative.

TAM/SAM/SOM should be calculated only after the population, serviceable geography, frequency, price and capacity assumptions are documented.
