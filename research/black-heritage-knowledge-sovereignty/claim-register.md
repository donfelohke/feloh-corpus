# Claim Register — Black Heritage & Knowledge Sovereignty

**Status:** RESEARCH / INITIAL — 26 October 2026  
**Rule:** This is a starter register, not a completed historical verdict. Each claim must be revisited as full sources and primary evidence are examined.

| ID | Claim / proposition | Status now | Evidence lead | Limits / counter-evidence needed | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BHKS-001 | The Roman province called Africa was initially a bounded region centred on present-day Tunisia, established after Carthage’s defeat in 146 BCE. | CORROBORATED at reference-summary level | Oxford, “Africa, Roman”; Oxford Bibliographies, “Roman Africa” | Exact boundaries and later provincial changes varied; the history of the name is longer than the original province. | Read complete entries and map changes by period. |
| BHKS-002 | The ultimate etymology of the word *Africa* is settled. | UNVERIFIED / likely overstatement | Carthage heritage page lists multiple hypotheses and says none is proven. | Public heritage summary is not specialist etymological consensus. | Review specialist historical linguistics and ancient sources. |
| BHKS-003 | Precolonial African societies were politically and culturally uniform. | DISPUTED / contradicted by broad comparative evidence | Comparative work on precolonial political organisation and trade documents regional variation. | Need regional and period-specific synthesis; avoid replacing uniformity with an equally broad claim of universal decentralisation. | Build a regional case-study matrix. |
| BHKS-004 | Trade connected African communities internally and with regions outside the continent. | CORROBORATED at broad level | Chirikure, “Documenting Precolonial Trade in Africa.” | Routes, commodities, participants, dates, and effects differed; some trade involved coercion and enslavement. | Extract region/time-specific evidence and primary sources. |
| BHKS-005 | Political decentralisation explains the success of all African trade networks. | UNVERIFIED / overgeneralised hypothesis | Chirikure; Henn and Robinson (2026); regional studies. | Trade operated under diverse institutions, including states and empires; causation must be tested rather than assumed. | Define “success” and compare case studies with counterexamples. |
| BHKS-006 | Pan-Africanism and all “Africanisms” fail on arrival because Africa lacked a singular identity. | OPINION / HYPOTHESIS, not established fact | Requires a defined body of scholarship and a measurable outcome. | Pan-Africanism has multiple intellectual, political, cultural, and institutional forms; cultural plurality alone does not establish failure. | Define the movement, time period, outcomes, and evaluation criteria; include counterexamples. |
| BHKS-007 | All Greek philosophy was copied from Egypt. | DISPUTED / unsupported as a blanket claim | Lefkowitz, “Egyptian philosophy: influence on ancient Greek thought”; competing scholarship and ancient sources required. | Contact, ancient reports, and demonstrated transmission are distinct evidence categories. | Compare claims philosopher by philosopher and doctrine by doctrine. |
| BHKS-008 | Egyptian–Greek intellectual contact and exchange are legitimate historical questions. | RESEARCH QUESTION supported by historiographical literature | Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry and its references. | Specific influence claims require independent examination. | Review primary texts, chronology, archaeology, and competing interpretations. |
| BHKS-009 | Persian survived unchanged after the Arab-Muslim conquest of Iran. | DISPUTED / inaccurate simplification | Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Persian Language i. Early New Persian.” | New Persian developed through linguistic, script, political, and literary transformation over centuries. | Build a language and institution timeline from specialist sources. |
| BHKS-010 | Persian language and literary culture continued and transformed after the conquest. | CORROBORATED at reference-summary level | Encyclopaedia Iranica; Oxford, “The Persian Cosmopolis.” | Regional histories differ; distinguish Persian language from modern Iranian national identity. | Review full chapters and primary textual evidence. |
| BHKS-011 | All North African populations became homogeneous or “Caucasianised” through Islam. | UNVERIFIED / overgeneralised and imprecise | Requires population-specific history; Islam is a religion, not a racial classification. | Collapses ancestry, language, religion, identity, and political history; ignores Berber/Amazigh, Arab, African, Mediterranean, and other complex histories. | Replace with bounded studies of migration, language, conversion, self-identification, and population history. |
| BHKS-012 | AI systems are being controlled specifically to prevent Black communities from reconstructing their history. | UNVERIFIED allegation | Requires direct evidence about identifiable actors, policies, decisions, and effects. | Platform concentration, bias, content moderation, and historical-data gaps are separate claims requiring separate evidence. | Investigate documented model/data limitations, moderation policies, access costs, and audit results; do not infer intent. |
| BHKS-013 | AI tools can help discover and connect dispersed historical sources. | PROPOSAL / capability hypothesis to test | Requires task-specific benchmarks and documented examples. | Hallucination, OCR errors, translation loss, dataset gaps, and fabricated citations can mislead. | Evaluate against expert-labelled corpora; measure retrieval precision, citation accuracy, language coverage, and error rates. |
| BHKS-014 | Community-controlled, federated technology can improve local control over knowledge infrastructure. | PROPOSAL | Technical and governance design work required. | Ownership alone does not guarantee security, inclusivity, sustainability, or good governance. | Specify threat model, operating costs, steward roles, portability tests, and dispute mechanisms. |
| BHKS-015 | Cultural plurality and technical interoperability can coexist. | INTERPRETATION / design principle | Open protocols and federated systems offer a plausible model; specific community outcomes require evaluation. | Interoperability can impose hidden standards or central dependencies; participation must remain voluntary and contestable. | Develop a reference architecture and test governance assumptions with case studies. |
| BHKS-016 | Privacy and accountability are mutually exclusive. | DISPUTED / false dichotomy as a design assumption | Privacy engineering and data-minimisation practice provide alternatives to indiscriminate collection. | Applicable legal duties vary by service and jurisdiction; no one design solves every compliance requirement. | Research Kenyan legal requirements and privacy-preserving verification with qualified review. |

## Interpretation boundary

The register deliberately separates historical facts from political interpretations and engineering proposals. A claim’s inclusion does not mean it is true. A status is provisional and may change only when the evidence record is updated.
