# Fieldwork protocol — Informal Economy in Eldoret and Uasin Gishu

**Status:** PROPOSED · requires review and local approvals before data collection  
**Version:** 0.1 · 25 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Safe, reproducible exploratory research. This protocol is not ethics approval, a representative sampling design, or evidence that fieldwork has occurred.

## 1. Principles

- Use official statistics for national context and local fieldwork for questions that cannot be answered at the required geographic or occupational level from those statistics.
- Begin with exploratory qualitative research. Do not claim statistical representativeness from convenience or purposive interviews.
- Include variation in occupation, worksite, work arrangement, business stage and digital access without assuming any one participant represents a group.
- Treat participants as knowledgeable about their work; avoid deficit framing.
- Keep research independent from sales, recruitment, enforcement and political campaigning.
- Determine whether institutional ethics review, county permission, market-management permission or other approvals are required before collection.

## 2. Phased method

### Phase A — Desk review and readiness
1. Extract relevant tables, definitions and limitations from KNBS Economic Survey 2026.
2. Review the Uasin Gishu CIDP, urban strategy, relevant annual development plans, budgets and county finance legislation.
3. Prepare participant information, consent script, interviewer safety plan, data management plan and referral/escalation procedure.
4. Default to no audio recording or photography unless justified and separately consented.
5. Set access controls, retention periods, deletion procedure and publication review for identifiability.

### Phase B — Site mapping
Build a candidate site register from public sources and preliminary observation. Candidate categories include markets, street-trading areas, retail clusters, workshops, food vending locations, transport nodes, beauty service providers, repair businesses and digital-work spaces.

These are **candidate categories, not verified site findings**. Before naming a location as a field site, verify its current name, general boundaries, access rules, conditions and safety. Do not publish a map of vulnerable individuals or precise unlicensed activity.

For each candidate site record a site code, publicly verifiable name/general area, source and date of verification, preliminary activity categories, access requirements, safety risks, public/restricted/private status, reason for inclusion and likely selection bias.

### Phase C — Pilot
Pilot the instrument only after approvals and consent materials are ready. Assess comprehension, duration, translation, safety and missing answer categories. Pilot responses are not population estimates. Log instrument changes and rationale.

### Phase D — Main exploratory fieldwork
Use a transparent purposive recruitment plan to seek variation across work types and settings. Where feasible, recruit through multiple routes rather than only personal contacts, one association or one market. Record aggregate recruitment limitations without unnecessary identifying information.

If population estimates are required, design a separate survey with an appropriate sampling frame and statistical support. Do not convert purposive interviews into percentages intended to represent Eldoret.

### Phase E — Analysis and validation
1. Maintain a codebook for themes and numeric variables.
2. Separate observation, participant statement, document-derived fact, interpretation and researcher inference.
3. Record contradictory cases and negative evidence.
4. For numeric results, report denominators, missingness, reference periods and uncertainty.
5. Invite participants or appropriate local groups to correct factual misunderstandings where safe, without granting any single party control over independent findings.
6. Publish aggregate findings, methods and limitations; keep identifiable raw records outside the public repository.

## 3. Exploratory coverage matrix

This is a planning aid, not a quota or claim about the local distribution of work.

| Dimension | Variation to seek | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Work type | Trading, food, repair/artisan work, beauty, transport/delivery, creative/digital work and locally identified categories | Avoid overfitting to one visible sector |
| Setting | Market stall, street/public space, fixed shop, home-based, mobile, shared workspace | Costs and exposure vary by setting |
| Work arrangement | Own-account, employer, paid worker, unpaid family worker, cooperative/member, platform-mediated | Workers and owners may face different risks |
| Enterprise stage | New, established, seasonal, changing or recently ceased (if voluntarily discussed) | Explore entry, survival and exit |
| Digital access | Smartphone, feature phone, shared device, intermittent connection, no regular digital access | Avoid assuming digital access is universal |
| Finance | Self-finance, household support, savings group, SACCO, supplier credit, formal/digital credit or other | Understand finance without collecting credentials |
| Perspectives | Operators, workers, associations, service providers and public officials | Triangulate rather than privileging one source |

## 4. Draft interview guide

Begin openly and use probes only when relevant.

### Work and enterprise
1. Can you tell me about the work you do and how you got started?
2. What does a typical working day or week look like?
3. Who does the work, including paid help or family assistance?
4. How does work change across seasons, weekdays or busy periods?

### Customers, suppliers and pricing
5. How do customers usually find you?
6. How do you decide what to charge?
7. Which inputs or services are difficult to obtain or change in price?
8. Do intermediaries or platforms affect access to customers or suppliers?

### Costs, cash flow and risk
9. What kinds of business costs do you have? Do not press for exact amounts.
10. How do you handle a day or week when sales are low?
11. What can delay payment or make it difficult to buy stock or materials?
12. Which risks are hardest to plan for?

### Finance and payments
13. Which payment methods do customers use, and what costs or difficulties arise?
14. What sources of working capital are available, if any?
15. What would make borrowing, saving or making payments safer or less costly?
16. Never ask for PINs, passwords, account numbers, loan identifiers or private transaction screenshots.

### Regulation, infrastructure and rights
17. Which permits, fees, services or rules affect your work, if any?
18. What is useful or difficult about the worksite, transport, storage, security, water, sanitation, electricity or connectivity?
19. When a dispute or safety problem occurs, what options are available?
20. What support or changes would be most useful, and who should be responsible?

### Technology and priorities
21. Which phones, apps, records or tools do you use for work?
22. What happens when a device breaks, power fails or the internet is unavailable?
23. What do those tools cost in money, time, fees or privacy?
24. What would you prefer to keep on paper, offline or under your own control?
25. If you could change one thing about how your work operates, what would it be?

Close by asking what was missed and whether follow-up contact is desired. Ask separately for contact details and store them only if necessary, with consent, in a restricted file. This draft is not a validated instrument; translate and test it with local language reviewers and pilot participants before field use.

## 5. Consent and privacy checklist

Before beginning, confirm the participant understands:
- the purpose, researcher identity, recording method and intended use;
- participation is voluntary and refusal carries no penalty;
- what may be quoted or published, including the risk that details can identify them;
- they can pause or stop and how withdrawal works;
- recording, photographs and future contact require separate consent.

Participation must not affect access to services, business opportunities or public benefits. Explain retention, access, withdrawal limits and publication plans. Do not promise anonymity if the design cannot deliver it.

Do not collect names, phone numbers, precise home locations, M-Pesa details, PINs, IDs, passwords, private customer lists, identifiable transaction screenshots or credentials in public research files. Separate contact/consent records from research data and restrict access. Use aggregation or suppress small groups where combinations of traits could identify someone. Do not commit identifiable raw records to this public repository.

Stop or postpone an interview if the participant appears pressured or distressed, a gatekeeper undermines voluntariness, the setting is unsafe, or the participant asks to stop. Do not confront authorities, photograph disputes, promise legal outcomes or record high-risk disclosures outside the approved protocol.

## 6. Minimum data dictionary

| Field | Definition | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| record_id | Random non-identifying record code | No initials, phone fragments or date-of-birth patterns |
| collection_date | Date of collection | ISO 8601; restrict exact dates if they increase identification risk |
| site_id | Approved site code | Link to restricted site register where necessary |
| sector_code | Broad work category | Document controlled vocabulary; allow other |
| work_arrangement | Participant-described work relationship | Do not infer legal status from job title |
| reference_period | Period covered by numeric answer | Required for sales, costs, hours and earnings |
| currency | Currency for amounts | Use ISO 4217 code, e.g. KES |
| amount_type | Sales, costs, debt payment, earnings or other | Never combine unlike quantities |
| evidence_type | Observation, participant statement, official source, estimate or interpretation | Required for each analytical claim |
| consent_status | Permitted scope of use | Store only necessary information; restrict access |
| review_status | Draft, checked, disputed, withheld or approved for publication | Publication approval is separate from collection |

## 7. Reporting template

Each finding must state:
- finding ID and exact wording;
- evidence class and source/record IDs;
- collection/publication dates and geographic/temporal scope;
- method, denominator and missingness where applicable;
- contradictory evidence and limitations;
- confidence rationale, not an unexplained numeric score;
- recommendation, responsible actor and trade-offs, if any;
- status: draft, checked, disputed, approved for publication or superseded.

## 8. Fieldwork readiness gate

Do not collect data until the following are checked:
- [ ] Purpose, questions and audience approved.
- [ ] Ethics review and local permissions determined and obtained where required.
- [ ] Consent and information materials reviewed in English and field languages.
- [ ] Recruitment plan and sampling limitations documented.
- [ ] Interviewer training and safety plan completed.
- [ ] Data minimisation, access, retention and deletion plan ready.
- [ ] Recording and photography justified and separately consented.
- [ ] Referral/escalation plan established.
- [ ] Pilot reviewed; instrument version logged.
- [ ] Public/private boundary checked; no identifiable raw data in GitHub.
