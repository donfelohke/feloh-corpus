## How to read this register

The status describes what has actually been inspected. An official text or catalogue record can verify the existence and wording/metadata of a document; it does not verify the truth of every claim made by its author or the implementation of a policy. Full-text review, exact passages and claim-specific annotation remain required before substantive conclusions are published.

## A. African regional law and continental policy

### MB-SOV-001 — African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights (1981)
- **Creator / custodian:** Organization of African Unity; African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights.
- **Type:** Regional human-rights treaty.
- **Official text:** https://achpr.au.int/en/charter/african-charter-human-and-peoples-rights/
- **Relevant provisions:** Articles 19–22 address equality of peoples, self-determination, natural resources and development.
- **Use:** Primary normative source for peoples' rights and the relationship between self-determination, resources and development.
- **Limitations:** Treaty text does not establish implementation or settle every question about the identity and scope of “peoples”. Relevant African Commission decisions and state practice must be reviewed.
- **Access status:** Official text inspected at article level; case-law review pending.

### MB-SOV-002 — Constitutive Act of the African Union (adopted 11 July 2000; entered into force 26 May 2001)
- **Creator / custodian:** African Union.
- **Type:** Founding treaty.
- **Official record:** https://au.int/en/treaties/constitutive-act-african-union
- **Relevant provisions:** Article 3 includes defence of member-state sovereignty, continental integration, popular participation, human rights, research and common positions. Article 4 includes sovereign equality and interdependence, participation of African peoples and self-reliance.
- **Use:** Primary source for the AU's institutional objectives and principles.
- **Limitations:** Distinguish treaty commitments from their implementation and from the separate rights of peoples under the African Charter.
- **Access status:** Official treaty record and text inspected.

### MB-SOV-003 — Agenda 2063: Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan, 2024–2033
- **Creator / custodian:** African Union / AUDA-NEPAD.
- **Type:** Continental policy and implementation framework.
- **Official plan page:** https://a2063.africa/publications/second-ten-year-implementation-plan-2024-to-2033
- **Launch-version PDF:** https://au.int/sites/default/files/newsevents/workingdocuments/43517-wd-Agenda_2063_STYIP_Feb_2024_Launch_Version.pdf
- **Use:** Contemporary policy framework for integration, development and the plan's seven Moonshots.
- **Limitations:** Aspirations, targets and implementation plans are not evidence of outcomes. Compare with monitoring reports, national data and independent assessments.
- **Access status:** Official plan page and PDF metadata inspected; full plan review pending.

## B. International law and peoples' rights

### MB-SOV-004 — Charter of the United Nations (1945), Chapter I, Article 1(2)
- **Creator / custodian:** United Nations.
- **Official text:** https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-1
- **Use:** Establishes the UN purpose of developing friendly relations based on equal rights and self-determination of peoples.
- **Limitations:** Interpret Article 1(2) in the context of the full Charter and subsequent international law.
- **Access status:** Official text inspected.

### MB-SOV-005 — International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966), Article 1
- **Creator / custodian:** United Nations; Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights.
- **Official text:** https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/ccpr.pdf
- **Use:** Common Article 1 recognises the right of all peoples to self-determination and addresses political status, development and natural resources.
- **Limitations:** Treaty obligations, state-party status and authoritative interpretation require separate review.
- **Access status:** Official text inspected at Article 1.

### MB-SOV-006 — International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (1966), Article 1
- **Creator / custodian:** United Nations; Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights.
- **Official text:** https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICESCR.html
- **Use:** Common Article 1 connects self-determination to economic, social and cultural development and natural resources.
- **Limitations:** Analyse the whole Covenant and its interpretive practice; do not isolate resource provisions from other obligations.
- **Access status:** Official text inspected at Article 1.

### MB-SOV-007 — Declaration on the Right to Development (UN General Assembly Resolution 41/128, 1986)
- **Creator / custodian:** United Nations General Assembly; OHCHR.
- **Official text:** https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/rtd.pdf
- **Relevant provisions:** Articles 1–3 address participation, self-determination, natural resources and state responsibility.
- **Use:** Normative framework linking development with active participation and peoples' rights.
- **Limitations:** A General Assembly declaration is not a treaty; distinguish normative influence from binding treaty obligations.
- **Access status:** Official text inspected at Articles 1–3.

### MB-SOV-008 — United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (A/RES/61/295, 2007)
- **Creator / custodian:** United Nations General Assembly.
- **Official text / overview:** https://www.un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples/declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples.html
- **Resolution record:** https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/606782/
- **Relevant provisions:** Articles 3–5, 11–13, 18–20 and 46 concern self-determination, autonomy, cultural rights, participation, institutions and territorial integrity.
- **Use:** Examine local and indigenous peoples' rights and the relationship between self-determination, autonomy and state integrity.
- **Limitations:** Study the Declaration's status and interpretation; do not assume every community or legal context is identical.
- **Access status:** UN official overview and resolution record inspected; full article-by-article review pending.

### MB-SOV-009 — Constitution of Kenya (2010)
- **Creator / custodian:** Republic of Kenya; official parliamentary publication.
- **Official text:** https://www.parliament.go.ke/sites/default/files/2023-03/The_Constitution_of_Kenya_2010.pdf
- **Relevant provisions:** Chapter One on sovereignty of the people; additional provisions on devolution, land and community land require detailed review.
- **Use:** Primary national case source for popular sovereignty and constitutional delegation at national and county levels.
- **Limitations:** Constitutional text is not evidence of lived implementation. Pair with statutes, judgments, official records and community evidence.
- **Access status:** Official parliamentary PDF inspected at Article 1; wider provisions pending.

## C. African philosophy and political thought

### MB-SOV-010 — Kwasi Wiredu, “Toward Decolonizing African Philosophy and Religion” (1998)
- **Creator:** Kwasi Wiredu.
- **Publication:** *African Studies Quarterly*, Volume 1, Issue 4 (1998).
- **Full text:** https://asq.africa.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/168/Vol-1-Issue-4.pdf
- **Use:** Critical conceptual decolonisation; Wiredu explicitly cautions against indiscriminately rejecting every idea with colonial origins.
- **Limitations:** One philosophical intervention, not a consensus statement. Compare with other African philosophers and critiques.
- **Access status:** Full article text located; detailed argument and page references pending.

### MB-SOV-011 — H. Odera Oruka (ed.), *Sage Philosophy: Indigenous Thinkers and Modern Debate on African Philosophy* (1990)
- **Creator / editor:** H. Odera Oruka.
- **Publisher:** E. J. Brill, Leiden / New York, 1990.
- **Bibliographic record:** https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/isbn/9789004452268/html
- **Use:** Kenyan sage philosophy, critical reasoning, interviews with sages and debates about African philosophy.
- **Limitations:** Review the book's method, interview contexts, selection and critics. Do not generalise from selected sages to every African knowledge tradition.
- **Access status:** Publisher catalogue metadata and description inspected; full book review pending.

### MB-SOV-012 — Amílcar Cabral, “National Liberation and Culture” (lecture delivered 20 February 1970)
- **Creator:** Amílcar Cabral.
- **Type:** Political and cultural lecture; English translation by Maureen Webster.
- **Accessible transcript:** https://www.historyisaweapon.com/defcon1/cabralnlac.html
- **Use:** Investigate the relationship between culture, organised resistance and national liberation.
- **Limitations:** The accessible transcript is hosted by a secondary archive, not the original publisher. Verify wording, translation and publication history against a reliable edition before quoting or relying on precise wording.
- **Access status:** Transcript located and metadata inspected; original edition verification pending.

### MB-SOV-013 — Kwame Nkrumah, *Neo-Colonialism: The Last Stage of Imperialism* (1965)
- **Creator:** Kwame Nkrumah.
- **Type:** Political-economic monograph.
- **Bibliographic record:** University of Eastern Africa, Baraton library catalogue: https://opac.ueab.ac.ke/cgi-bin/koha/opac-detail.pl?biblionumber=44748
- **Use:** Historical argument about external economic influence and formal versus substantive independence.
- **Limitations:** Treat as a situated political argument. Inspect the full work, its evidence and subsequent critiques; do not generalise it to every contemporary external relationship.
- **Access status:** Library catalogue metadata inspected; full text review pending.
